Here is how each of those four looks in practice.

## 1. A visible batch report

A certificate of analysis (COA) is a lab report for one batch. What buyers want is to see it before they pay. Enzo Meds says every product is lab tested, every batch is verified and the COA sits on the product listing.

Once you have the report open, check three things: the compound name, the purity figure and the batch number. When the parcel arrives, match the batch number to your label. Our guide to [reading a peptide certificate of analysis](/posts/batch-03/how-to-read-a-peptide-certificate-of-analysis/) explains each line.

## 2. A person who answers

Ask a company a question and see how it responds. Enzo Meds offers 1:1 support on WhatsApp, Telegram and a website contact form, and says it replies within minutes during business hours.

Try messages such as:

- "Which size suits a plan of 12 weeks?"
- "Can it arrive by Friday?"
- "Where is the COA for this batch?"

The quality of a reply tells you how the company treats its customers.

## Keep your own record

Whatever you buy, a simple record helps you across repeat orders. Write down:

| Item | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| Order number | Confirmation email |
| Batch number | Product label and COA |
| Order date | Confirmation email |
| First-use date | Your notes |

Over a few orders you will see the same details each time, which is a good sign you are dealing with a shop that keeps its batches organised.
