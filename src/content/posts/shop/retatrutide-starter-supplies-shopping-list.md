## Alcohol swabs

Use 70 percent isopropyl alcohol swabs. Wipe the top of a vial and let it dry before piercing it. Wipe the skin at the site too. Twenty swabs cover four weeks with spares. See [where to inject retatrutide](/posts/batch-03/where-to-inject-retatrutide/) for site rotation.

## Sharps bin

Used needles go in a rigid container with a lid. A 0.5 to 1 litre bin covers a few months of weekly use. Ask your local council or pharmacy how to hand it in when it is about three quarters full. Never put loose needles in a household bin.

## Fridge storage

A small lidded box keeps items upright and together. Put it on a shelf away from the back wall, the freezer compartment and the door. Keep it out of light. For the general rules, read [how to store reconstituted peptides](/posts/batch-03/how-to-store-reconstituted-peptides/), and follow any storage note on the product page first.

## If your product needs mixing

Some peptides arrive as freeze-dried powder that has to be mixed. In that case you need these:

### Bacteriostatic (BAC) water

BAC water is sterile water with 0.9 percent benzyl alcohol as a preservative. It is used to mix freeze-dried peptide and can be drawn from more than once. Date the vial when you first pierce it. See [How to Use BAC Water With Retatrutide](/posts/batch-02/how-to-use-bac-water-with-retatrutide/).

### Syringes

Insulin syringes are marked in units, where 100 units equal 1 mL. Common sizes are 0.3 mL, 0.5 mL and 1 mL. A smaller barrel has finer markings. Work out your mixing volume with [how much BAC water for a 15 mg retatrutide vial](/posts/batch-02/how-much-bac-water-for-15-mg-retatrutide/). Use each syringe once.

## Common questions about supplies

### How many syringes do I need?

One per dose, plus at least two spares. A box of 10 or 30 usually costs less per syringe than buying singly.

### Do I need a special fridge?

No. A standard fridge works. Use a lidded box on a shelf and keep it away from the freezer compartment.
