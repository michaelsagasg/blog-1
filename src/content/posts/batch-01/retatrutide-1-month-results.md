At one month, weight loss on retatrutide in trials is small compared with the 48-week totals. Doses start low, so the first four weeks are mostly about appetite changes and side effects, not big scale changes.

The phase 2 trial did not publish a single one-month figure as a headline, so any exact number for week 4 is an estimate. What it does show is the shape of the curve.

## Why month one is slow

Trial participants did not begin at the top dose. In the phase 2 study, the higher-dose groups started at 2 mg and stepped up every four weeks. The 12 mg group needed several steps to reach its full dose. So in month one, everyone was on a low dose.

Weight loss depends on dose, and the low starting dose explains why the early scale change is modest.

## The shape of the curve

Phase 2 average weight loss for the 12 mg group:

| Time | Average weight loss |
|---|---|
| Week 24 | 17.5% |
| Week 48 | 24.2% |

Loss was fastest in the first half and continued after that. The month one figure sits well below the week 24 figure. The trial's published charts show the line rising steadily from the start.

## What people notice in the first month

Reported early effects on drugs of this type include:

- **Lower appetite.** Many people feel full sooner and think about food less.
- **Nausea.** Usually mild, often worse right after a dose increase.
- **Changes in bowel habits.** Constipation or loose stools.
- **Fatigue.** Often linked to eating less.

None of these predict how much weight you will lose later. Some people have strong early effects and average results, and the reverse also happens.

## Why the scale can mislead early on

Weight in the first weeks includes water and gut contents, not only fat. Eating less carbohydrate and less food overall lowers stored water. That can make early changes look bigger or smaller than the fat change underneath.

Measure weight the same way each time, such as the same scale, the same morning time, and a weekly average.

## Month one and safety

The first weeks matter for tolerability:

- Early stomach symptoms are most common after dose steps.
- Heart rate rose on average in trials and peaked around week 24.
- Dehydration from vomiting or diarrhea is the most common reason for urgent care with GLP-1 based drugs.

Anyone taking a prescribed GLP-1 medicine should tell their clinician about symptoms that do not settle.

## A caution about unverified products

Retatrutide is not approved anywhere. Trial doses were made and monitored under pharmaceutical controls. Products bought online may contain a different dose or a different substance, so one-month results posted online do not map cleanly to trial data.

## Questions people ask about the first month

### Is it normal to lose little in month one?

Yes. Low starting doses and slow escalation mean the first month is the smallest part of the total. A loss of a few pounds is typical for a drug that later averages more than 20% of body weight.

### Should I judge the drug after four weeks?

No. In the phase 2 trial, the gap between placebo and the higher doses kept widening for the full 48 weeks. Four weeks is too early to tell a strong responder from an average one.

### What matters more than the scale?

Tolerability. A dose you can stay on for months beats one that makes you quit in week three. Trial teams used slow escalation for this reason.

## Summary

- Month one is a low-dose period in every retatrutide trial.
- The phase 2 data shows 17.5% at 24 weeks and 24.2% at 48 weeks on 12 mg.
- Early weight change is modest and includes water.
- Side effects tend to peak after dose steps.
- One-month results from unverified products are not comparable to trial results.

Judge the drug on the full timeline, not the first month.
