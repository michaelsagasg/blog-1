The most common complaints about retatrutide are stomach-related: nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, and constipation. They rise with dose and usually show up during dose increases. Trials also reported a rise in heart rate and, at higher doses, unusual skin sensations.

This post covers what published trials found. It is not a review of a product, because retatrutide is not approved and is only given in clinical studies.

## Stomach side effects

In the phase 2 trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2023, gastrointestinal events were the main reported problem. They were mostly mild to moderate, and they were more frequent at higher doses and with faster dose increases.

Typical pattern reported:

- Nausea peaks after a dose step and fades over the following weeks.
- Diarrhea and constipation can both occur, sometimes in the same person.
- Vomiting is less common than nausea but rises at the top doses.

Trial designers responded by using slower escalation. The same lesson came from semaglutide and tirzepatide, where gradual increases cut down on early dropouts.

## Heart rate

Retatrutide raised resting heart rate in the phase 2 trial. The increase peaked around week 24 and then came down. Other GLP-1 based drugs also raise heart rate a little, and retatrutide's glucagon action may add to that. Researchers followed it because heart rate changes matter for people with heart conditions.

## Skin sensation changes (dysesthesia)

In the phase 3 TRIUMPH-4 results reported by Eli Lilly, a share of participants on higher doses reported dysesthesia, which is an altered or uncomfortable skin sensation, such as tingling or sensitivity to touch. It was reported more often at 12 mg than at lower doses. Most cases were described as mild or moderate. This is a new signal that earlier trials of related drugs did not highlight, so full data will matter.

## Stopping treatment because of side effects

Higher doses had more participants leaving the trial because of side effects. Some of these were people who lost weight very quickly and no longer wanted to lose more. Lilly noted that dose choice and the amount of weight lost interact, and it is studying that.

## Other things to know

### Rapid weight loss has its own effects

Losing 20% or more of body weight in under a year can cause loss of lean mass, gallbladder problems, hair shedding, and fatigue. These are seen with other strong weight-loss drugs too.

### Hydration and food

Many people on GLP-1 based drugs eat less, and some drink less. Dehydration from vomiting or diarrhea is a common reason people need medical care.

## Comparing complaints across drugs

| Effect | Semaglutide | Tirzepatide | Retatrutide (trials) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nausea | Common | Common | Common, dose-related |
| Diarrhea | Common | Common | Common, dose-related |
| Heart rate rise | Small | Small | Larger, peaks around week 24 |
| Skin sensation change | Not a highlight | Not a highlight | Reported at higher doses |

These are qualitative and come from different trials. They are not a head-to-head comparison.

## What reviews you find online cannot tell you

Online reviews mix people using unverified products, unknown doses, and other medicines. A symptom listed in a review may come from any of those. Trial data is cleaner because dose, product, and monitoring are known.

## When to talk to a clinician

Get medical advice for any of these while on a GLP-1 based medicine:

- Severe or constant stomach pain.
- Vomiting that prevents you from keeping fluids down.
- Fast heartbeat or chest symptoms.
- Yellowing of the skin or eyes.
- Any symptom that worries you.

## How complaints change over time

Stomach effects tend to be worst in the first weeks and after each dose step, then ease. Trials that raised the dose more slowly saw fewer early dropouts. This is the same pattern seen with approved GLP-1 medicines, and it is why escalation schedules exist.

Complaints that persist or get worse over time are different from early adjustment effects. Those are the ones to raise with a clinician.

## Summary

- Stomach effects are the most common complaint and rise with dose.
- Heart rate goes up on average and peaks around week 24.
- Skin sensation changes appeared at higher doses in phase 3.
- Slower dose escalation reduces early problems.
- Retatrutide is still investigational, and long-term safety is unknown.
