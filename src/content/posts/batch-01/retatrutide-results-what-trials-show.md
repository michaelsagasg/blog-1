Retatrutide has produced the largest average weight loss of any obesity drug tested so far. In a 48-week phase 2 trial, adults on the top dose lost an average of 24.2% of their body weight. In a phase 3 study, the top dose reached 28.7% at 68 weeks.

Those are averages from tightly run trials. Your own result depends on your dose, your starting weight, how long you stay on it, and how your body responds.

## The phase 2 result

The phase 2 trial was published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2023. It enrolled 338 adults with obesity or overweight. Participants injected retatrutide or placebo once a week for 48 weeks.

Average weight change at 48 weeks:

| Group | Average weight loss |
|---|---|
| Placebo | 2.1% |
| 1 mg | 8.7% |
| 4 mg | 17.1% |
| 8 mg | 22.8% |
| 12 mg | 24.2% |

At 24 weeks, the 12 mg group had lost 17.5%. The curve had not flattened by week 48, which means people were still losing weight when the trial ended.

In the 12 mg group, 100% of participants lost at least 5% of their body weight. Most lost at least 15%, and about half lost 25% or more.

## The phase 3 result

Eli Lilly reported the first phase 3 data from a trial called TRIUMPH-4. It enrolled adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis. At 68 weeks, the 12 mg group lost an average of 28.7% of body weight, about 71 pounds. Pain scores in the knee also dropped.

This was a study in a specific group, so it is not a perfect preview of every future trial. More phase 3 results are expected as the other TRIUMPH studies finish.

## How it compares

Here is how the headline numbers line up with the two best-known approved drugs. These come from separate trials with different lengths and populations, so treat the comparison as rough.

| Drug | Trial length | Average weight loss (top dose) |
|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide 2.4 mg | 68 weeks | about 15% |
| Tirzepatide 15 mg | 72 weeks | about 21% |
| Retatrutide 12 mg | 48 weeks | 24.2% |

Retatrutide targets three hormone receptors: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. Semaglutide targets one, and tirzepatide targets two. The glucagon piece is thought to raise energy use and help the liver burn fat.

## What the averages hide

An average is a middle point. Some people lost far more, and some lost far less. In the phase 2 trial, weight loss rose with dose, and the higher doses came with more side effects.

Three details matter when you read any result:

- **Dose escalation.** Trial participants started low and stepped up over weeks. Nobody started at the top dose.
- **Lifestyle support.** Trial participants got diet and activity counseling.
- **Selection.** People who join trials are screened, monitored, and motivated.

## What retatrutide results do not include

Retatrutide is not approved by the FDA or any other regulator. The results above come from clinical trials, where the drug was made, dosed, and monitored under controlled conditions. Long-term safety data does not exist yet.

The phase 2 trial ran 48 weeks. The phase 3 program is longer, but full results on cardiovascular outcomes, weight regain after stopping, and rare side effects are still to come.

## Reading the fine print on trial results

Two details change how you should read any trial figure.

First, results are often reported as "efficacy estimand" or "treatment-regimen estimand." The first describes people who stayed on the drug. The second includes everyone, even those who stopped. The first number is usually higher. Check which one a headline uses.

Second, trials report percent of starting body weight, not pounds. A 25% loss is about 55 pounds for someone who starts at 220 pounds and about 75 pounds for someone who starts at 300.

## The short version

- Phase 2, 12 mg, 48 weeks: 24.2% average weight loss.
- Phase 3 TRIUMPH-4, 12 mg, 68 weeks: 28.7% average weight loss.
- Weight loss was dose-dependent and still rising at the end of the phase 2 trial.
- Side effects were also dose-dependent, mostly stomach-related.
- The drug is still investigational.

If you want a realistic picture, read the trial tables, not the highlights. The numbers are impressive, and they still describe a group average under trial conditions.
