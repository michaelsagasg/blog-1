The first phase 3 result for retatrutide came from TRIUMPH-4. Adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis who took 12 mg once weekly lost an average of 28.7% of their body weight over 68 weeks, about 71 pounds. Knee pain also fell.

That is the largest average weight loss reported in a phase 3 obesity trial to date.

## What TRIUMPH-4 tested

TRIUMPH-4 enrolled adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis. The two goals were weight loss and knee pain. Participants injected retatrutide or placebo once a week for 68 weeks.

The dose groups included 9 mg and 12 mg. Eli Lilly announced the topline results in a press release, with full data to be presented and published later.

## Headline numbers

| Measure | Retatrutide 12 mg |
|---|---|
| Average weight loss | 28.7% |
| Average pounds lost | about 71 |
| Duration | 68 weeks |
| Population | Obesity plus knee osteoarthritis |

Placebo participants lost a small fraction of that. Knee pain scores, measured with a standard osteoarthritis questionnaire, dropped substantially in the retatrutide groups.

## How it fits with phase 2

The phase 2 trial found 24.2% weight loss at 48 weeks on 12 mg. Phase 3 ran 20 weeks longer and reached a higher average. That fits the pattern from phase 2, where weight was still falling when the trial stopped.

Longer treatment, larger groups, and a defined population make phase 3 more reliable than phase 2, but this is a single trial in a specific group.

## Safety in phase 3

Most side effects were stomach-related, as before. A new point was dysesthesia, an altered skin sensation, reported by a larger share of people on higher doses. Discontinuations because of side effects were higher at 12 mg than at lower doses. Lilly said that in some cases this reflected people reaching a weight where they did not want to lose more.

These details will be clearer once full data is released.

## What phase 3 still has to answer

TRIUMPH-4 is one of several phase 3 studies. Others look at different groups:

- Adults with obesity or overweight and related conditions.
- Adults with type 2 diabetes.
- Obstructive sleep apnea.
- Cardiovascular and kidney outcomes.

The full program will show whether the results hold in other populations and whether the drug lowers heart disease risk.

## What happens next

A drug goes through several steps after phase 3:

1. Complete the remaining phase 3 trials.
2. Submit results to the FDA and other regulators.
3. Regulators review the data, which typically takes months.
4. If approved, the drug gets a label with approved doses and uses.

Retatrutide has not reached step 2 at the time of writing. Approval is not guaranteed, and timing can change.

## What this means for readers

- The efficacy number is strong: 28.7% at 68 weeks on the top dose.
- The safety picture is still being filled in.
- Retatrutide is investigational and not available by prescription.
- Products sold online as retatrutide are not the drug used in these trials.

## Why knee osteoarthritis was chosen

Excess weight loads the knees, and weight loss is one of the few treatments known to reduce osteoarthritis pain. Testing retatrutide here gave researchers two readouts in one trial: how much weight people lost, and whether that translated into less pain and better function.

The pain result matters because it shows a benefit people can feel, separate from the number on the scale.

## How to read a press release result

A topline announcement gives headline averages. It leaves out the detail that peer review checks, such as how missing data was handled, how many people stopped treatment, and how results split by subgroup. Treat the figures as a strong first look, and expect the published paper to add caveats.

## Who the result applies to

Everyone in TRIUMPH-4 had obesity and knee osteoarthritis. People with type 2 diabetes usually lose less weight on the same drugs, so the figure may not carry over to them. Other TRIUMPH trials test those groups directly, and their results will show how far 28.7% travels.

## Bottom line

TRIUMPH-4 confirmed that the large weight loss in phase 2 carries into a bigger, longer trial. It also raised a new side effect to watch. Wait for the full data and the other phase 3 studies before drawing conclusions.
