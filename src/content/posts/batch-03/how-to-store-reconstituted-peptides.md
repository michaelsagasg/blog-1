Reconstituted peptides are generally stored in the refrigerator at 2 to 8 °C (36 to 46 °F), protected from light, and used within about 28 days when mixed with bacteriostatic water. Unmixed freeze-dried powder is more stable and is often stored in a cold, dry place until needed.

Exact rules depend on the specific peptide and its stability data. Approved drug labels are the best source, and research products have no reliable stability data.

## Powder vs liquid

Freeze-dried (lyophilized) peptide is stable because water is removed. Once mixed, the peptide sits in solution, where several things happen:

- **Hydrolysis and oxidation** slowly break down the molecule.
- **Aggregation** can cause clumping and lower potency.
- **Bacteria** can grow if the vial is contaminated.
- **Adsorption** to plastic or glass can reduce the amount in solution.

Solutions degrade faster than powder, so shelf life is short.

## Fridge storage steps

1. **Refrigerate right after mixing.** Do not leave the vial out for hours.
2. **Keep it at 2 to 8 °C.** A main shelf is better than a door, where temperatures swing.
3. **Keep it upright and capped.** Return it to the fridge after each use.
4. **Protect from light.** Keep the vial in its box or a dark container.
5. **Do not freeze.** Freezing can damage many peptides and vials. Check guidance for a specific product.
6. **Label with the mix date and concentration.**

## Shelf life

| Situation | Typical guidance |
|---|---|
| Lyophilized powder, unopened, cold | Months to years, per product label |
| Reconstituted with bacteriostatic water, refrigerated | Often up to about 28 days |
| Reconstituted with sterile water, no preservative | Use within a day or a few days |
| Left at room temperature | Hours to a few days at most, and only as the label allows |

Approved prefilled pens, such as semaglutide or tirzepatide, list room-temperature allowances, for example up to 21 days for some products. Those are specific to the formulation and do not apply to other peptides.

## Why bacteriostatic water is used

Bacteriostatic water contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol, which slows bacterial growth, allowing multiple withdrawals from one vial over weeks. Plain sterile water has no preservative and is meant for single use. Bacteriostatic water is not suitable for newborns and should be used carefully in people sensitive to benzyl alcohol.

## Handling to prevent contamination

- Wash hands and use gloves if you prefer.
- Wipe the rubber stopper with a fresh alcohol swab before each draw.
- Use a new sterile syringe and needle each time.
- Never touch the needle or stopper.
- Do not shake the vial. Roll it gently to mix.
- Discard the vial if it looks cloudy, has particles, or changes color.

## Signs a vial has gone bad

- Cloudiness or floating particles
- Color change
- Slime or film
- Unusual smell
- A vial that was left out of the fridge for a long period

When in doubt, throw it out. Injecting a contaminated product can cause an abscess or systemic infection.

## Travel

Use an insulated pouch with a cold pack, and avoid direct contact between the vial and ice packs to prevent freezing. Keep prescription drugs in original packaging where required and carry documentation. Airlines allow medical injectables, and rules vary.

## Discarding

Use a sharps container for needles and syringes. Local rules for disposing of vials vary.

## Availability

Retatrutide is investigational and has no official storage guidance. The advice here reflects general practice for reconstituted peptides.

## Concentration and dose after storage

Storage affects potency. A peptide that has lost 10% of its activity gives a smaller dose than the math suggests, and the loss is not visible. Keeping records helps: write the mix date on the vial, and consider replacing vials earlier than the maximum window, especially for products with no stability data.

## Why not freeze aliquots?

Some people split reconstituted solution into small vials and freeze them. Repeated freeze-thaw cycles can damage peptides, and plastic tubes can adsorb peptide. Where freezing is allowed by a manufacturer, single-use aliquots that are thawed once are the usual approach. For most injectable products, refrigeration is the labeled method, so follow the label.

## Temperature excursions

If a vial was left at room temperature for a few hours, it is often still usable, but each excursion shortens shelf life. If it has been in a hot car or sun for hours, discard it. Use a fridge thermometer to check that temperatures stay in range, since some fridges run cold enough to freeze products at the back.

## Bottom line

Keep reconstituted peptides cold, dark, and clean, mark the mix date, and discard after about 28 days or sooner if anything looks off.
