Retatrutide has produced results in four main areas so far: obesity, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver disease, and knee osteoarthritis with obesity. The largest number is 28.7% average weight loss at 68 weeks in the phase 3 TRIUMPH-4 trial.

This post lists each readout, what it measured, and how strong the evidence is.

## Phase 2 in obesity

This was a 48-week trial in adults with obesity or overweight plus a weight-related condition. It was published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2023.

| Group | Average weight change at 48 weeks |
|---|---|
| Placebo | -2.1% |
| Retatrutide 1 mg | -8.7% |
| Retatrutide 4 mg | -17.1% |
| Retatrutide 8 mg | -22.8% |
| Retatrutide 12 mg | -24.2% |

The results rose with dose. Weight was still falling at 48 weeks in the higher groups.

## Phase 2 in type 2 diabetes

A separate 24-week trial enrolled adults with type 2 diabetes. The highest dose lowered HbA1c by about 2 percentage points, and weight loss reached about 17% at 24 weeks. Both were larger than the comparison drug, dulaglutide, in that trial.

Weight loss is lower in type 2 diabetes than in people without it, across all drugs in this class.

## Phase 2 in fatty liver

A sub-study measured liver fat in people with fatty liver disease. Liver fat fell sharply, and in the two highest dose groups a large majority reached a normal level of liver fat by 24 weeks. This was a small imaging study, so it needs confirmation in larger trials.

## Phase 3 TRIUMPH-4

The first phase 3 readout enrolled adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis:

- 12 mg: 28.7% average weight loss at 68 weeks, about 71 lb.
- Knee pain scores fell substantially.
- Placebo participants lost only a small amount.

Eli Lilly announced topline results, with full data to follow.

## Safety across trials

The pattern has been consistent:

- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation were the most common side effects, mostly mild to moderate and most frequent during dose increases.
- Heart rate rose in a dose-dependent way, peaking around 24 weeks and then declining.
- Skin sensation changes (dysesthesia) were reported more often at higher doses in phase 3.
- Discontinuation due to side effects was higher at higher doses.

## What has not been shown yet

- Long-term safety beyond about 68 weeks.
- Effects on heart attacks, strokes, and kidney disease. These outcome trials are in the phase 3 program.
- How results hold up after stopping.
- Direct head-to-head data against tirzepatide across a full program.

## How to weigh the evidence

Phase 2 trials are small, with a few hundred people in total across dose groups. Phase 3 is larger and more reliable. Topline press releases give averages but leave out detail such as how dropouts were handled.

The direction is consistent across studies: large weight loss, better blood sugar, and less liver fat. The open questions are about safety over years, not whether the drug works in the short term.

## Status

Retatrutide is investigational and unapproved. Regulators have not reviewed a full application at the time of writing. Nothing in these trial results applies to products sold as research chemicals.

## Phase 3 beyond TRIUMPH-4

TRIUMPH-4 is the first phase 3 readout, and more are in the pipeline. The wider TRIUMPH program covers obesity with related conditions, obstructive sleep apnea, and cardiovascular and kidney outcomes. A separate TRANSCEND program covers type 2 diabetes. Each will add data for its own population.

## How the pieces fit together

Put the studies side by side and a consistent story appears:

1. Phase 2 showed a dose response, with more drug giving more weight loss.
2. The diabetes trial showed blood sugar falls sharply along with weight.
3. The liver study showed that the fat lost includes fat stored in the liver.
4. Phase 3 confirmed the size of the effect in a bigger, longer trial.

Consistency across trials is what makes a result believable. A one-off number can be luck. The same direction across four different studies is harder to explain away.

## Comparing with other drugs

Cross-trial comparisons are unreliable because populations and durations differ. Still, for orientation:

| Drug | Trial | Average weight loss |
|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide 2.4 mg | STEP 1, 68 weeks | about 15% |
| Tirzepatide 15 mg | SURMOUNT-1, 72 weeks | about 21% |
| Retatrutide 12 mg | TRIUMPH-4, 68 weeks | 28.7% |

Head-to-head trials are the only clean way to compare, and none has been reported for retatrutide.

## Bottom line

The trial record shows a drug that lowers weight more than anything tested at this scale so far, with a familiar side-effect profile and a few new points to watch. The rest of phase 3 will settle whether those results hold in more people and over more time.
