Retatrutide and tirzepatide both mimic gut hormones, but retatrutide hits a third target, the glucagon receptor. In separate trials, retatrutide 12 mg produced 24.2% weight loss at 48 weeks and 28.7% at 68 weeks, while tirzepatide 15 mg produced about 21% at 72 weeks. No head-to-head trial has been reported.

## Side-by-side

| Feature | Tirzepatide | Retatrutide |
|---|---|---|
| Targets | GIP and GLP-1 | GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon |
| Brand names | Mounjaro, Zepbound | None (investigational) |
| Dosing | Once weekly | Once weekly in trials |
| Top trial dose | 15 mg | 12 mg |
| Average loss in main obesity trial | about 21% at 72 weeks | 24.2% at 48 weeks; 28.7% at 68 weeks |
| Status | Approved | Phase 3 |
| Company | Eli Lilly | Eli Lilly |

## Why the third target matters

Glucagon signaling raises energy expenditure and helps burn fat in the liver. In theory, it adds to the appetite effect from GLP-1 and the metabolic effects of GIP. The higher weight loss in phase 2 and phase 3 fits that idea, though it is hard to prove how much comes from each pathway.

## Comparing the trials

Cross-trial comparisons are unreliable. Differences include:

- **Population.** SURMOUNT-1 enrolled adults with obesity or overweight and related conditions. TRIUMPH-4 enrolled adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis.
- **Duration.** 72 weeks versus 68 weeks, or 48 weeks in phase 2.
- **Estimand.** Whether dropouts are counted in the average.
- **Dose escalation.** Different schedules.

A head-to-head trial would remove these issues. Lilly has run one for tirzepatide against semaglutide (SURMOUNT-5), which favored tirzepatide.

## Side effects

Both drugs cause nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation, mostly during dose increases. Specific differences so far:

- Retatrutide raised resting heart rate in a dose-related way in phase 2, peaking around week 24.
- Retatrutide phase 3 reported more dysesthesia at higher doses.
- Tirzepatide has a large safety database from years of use, including in people with diabetes and heart disease.

Retatrutide's safety picture is less complete simply because fewer people have taken it.

## Diabetes

Both drugs lower blood sugar. Tirzepatide is approved for type 2 diabetes as Mounjaro. Retatrutide's phase 2 trial showed HbA1c drops of about 2 points at 24 weeks, and phase 3 diabetes trials are ongoing.

## Availability

Tirzepatide is prescription-only and available in the US and other countries. Retatrutide is not approved anywhere. Products sold online as retatrutide are not the trial drug.

## Who might prefer which

For now the question is theoretical, since only one is approved. If retatrutide is approved, factors that would matter include:

1. How much weight you need to lose.
2. Tolerance of side effects.
3. Heart rate and cardiovascular history.
4. Price and coverage.
5. Long-term safety data.

## What we still need to see

- A head-to-head trial with tirzepatide.
- Retatrutide cardiovascular outcomes.
- Data on maintenance doses.
- Real-world dropout rates.

## Head-to-head evidence to date

The closest evidence of how newer drugs compare comes from SURMOUNT-5, a trial of tirzepatide versus semaglutide in adults with obesity. Tirzepatide produced about 20% weight loss at 72 weeks versus about 14% for semaglutide. That result shows that head-to-head trials can put numbers on differences that cross-trial comparisons only hint at. A similar trial for retatrutide against tirzepatide would be the natural next step.

## Practical differences that matter to patients

- **Number of years on the market.** Tirzepatide has been used for years in diabetes and obesity, so rare side effects are better known.
- **Pen versus vial.** Tirzepatide comes in prefilled pens with fixed doses. Retatrutide's trial form has been supplied by the sponsor, and a commercial form is unknown.
- **Supply.** Tirzepatide has had shortages. A new drug would face supply limits at launch.
- **Insurance.** Coverage for obesity drugs varies widely, and a newer, more powerful drug may cost more.

## Bottom line

Retatrutide looks more potent than tirzepatide in early trials, likely because of the added glucagon action. Tirzepatide has the advantages of approval and years of safety data, so the comparison will only be settled by head-to-head evidence and time.
