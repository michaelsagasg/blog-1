The best before-and-after data for retatrutide comes from clinical trials, not photos. In the phase 2 obesity trial, people on the 12 mg dose lost an average of 24.2% of their body weight in 48 weeks. In TRIUMPH-4, the 12 mg group lost an average of 28.7% in 68 weeks, about 71 pounds.

Those are averages. Individual results ran higher and lower.

## Converting percentages to pounds

A percentage means little until you apply it to a starting weight. Here is what the two headline averages look like.

| Starting weight | 24.2% loss (phase 2, 48 weeks) | 28.7% loss (TRIUMPH-4, 68 weeks) |
|---|---|---|
| 200 lb | 48 lb | 57 lb |
| 230 lb | 56 lb | 66 lb |
| 250 lb | 60 lb | 72 lb |
| 300 lb | 73 lb | 86 lb |

TRIUMPH-4 participants started at roughly 250 lb on average, which is why 28.7% equals about 71 pounds.

## What the placebo group looked like

The comparison matters as much as the result. In phase 2, people on placebo lost 2.1% of their weight over 48 weeks. That is roughly 5 pounds for a 230 lb person. The retatrutide groups were not just lower, they were in a different range altogether.

Every trial participant also received lifestyle counseling. So the placebo number shows what diet advice alone produced in that setting.

## The shape of the curve

Weight loss in the trials was not a straight line. Most of the drop came in the first several months, then the curve flattened. In phase 2, weight was still falling at 48 weeks in the higher dose groups, which is why the longer phase 3 trial reached a higher average.

A rough pattern from the phase 2 data:

- Weeks 1 to 8: small doses, slow start, mostly appetite changes.
- Weeks 8 to 24: the steepest part of the curve as the dose rises.
- Weeks 24 to 48: slower loss that had not fully plateaued at the top dose.

## Why photos are not evidence

Online before-and-after photos have three problems:

1. Selection. People post their best results, not the median.
2. No control. Lighting, posture, and time between photos are unmatched.
3. Unknown product. Many products sold online as retatrutide are unverified, so a photo cannot tell you what someone actually took.

A trial reports every participant, including those who stopped early or lost little.

## Spread around the average

Not everyone landed at 24% or 28%. In phase 2, the share of people losing at least 15% of body weight at 48 weeks was very high at the top doses, and a large share lost 25% or more. Some people lost far less. Trials also count people who quit because of side effects, which pulls averages down.

If you are trying to picture your own range, think of the average as the middle, with a wide band on either side.

## Body composition, not only weight

Weight includes fat, muscle, and water. In the phase 2 trial, a sub-study measured body composition and found that most of the lost weight was fat mass, with the rest lean mass. That split is similar to other weight-loss drugs. Strength training and enough protein help protect muscle, and a before-and-after photo cannot show that difference.

## Other changes measured

Trials tracked more than the scale. Phase 2 and later studies reported changes in:

- Waist circumference
- Blood pressure
- Triglycerides and other blood fats
- Blood sugar in people with type 2 diabetes
- Liver fat in people with fatty liver disease

These are often the changes doctors care about most.

## Who the results apply to

The phase 2 trial enrolled adults with obesity, or with overweight and at least one weight-related condition. TRIUMPH-4 enrolled adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis. Results in people with type 2 diabetes tend to be smaller for every drug in this class. Do not apply an obesity trial number to a different group.

## Availability

Retatrutide is investigational. It is not approved anywhere and is not available by prescription. The trial drug was made and dosed under controlled conditions, and it cannot be assumed that products sold online match it.

## Questions people ask about the before-and-after numbers

### Is 28.7% typical or the best case?

It is the average for the 12 mg group in one trial, so about half of participants lost less and half lost more. The group also had a specific profile: obesity plus knee osteoarthritis. It is a fair anchor, but not a floor.

### Do people keep the weight off?

Trials of related drugs show regain after stopping. In the tirzepatide SURMOUNT-4 trial, people who switched to placebo after 36 weeks regained about 14% of their body weight over the next year, while those who continued kept losing. Retatrutide has not published a comparable stopping trial in full, so expect a similar pattern.

### How should you measure your own progress?

If you ever take part in a supervised program, track more than the scale:

- Weight on the same scale, same time of day, once a week.
- Waist measurement at the navel.
- Strength, such as how much you lift or how many stairs you climb without stopping.
- Lab values your clinician orders, such as HbA1c and lipids.

A single-week change means little because water shifts by a few pounds. Look at four-week trends.

## Bottom line

The before-and-after picture from trials is an average loss of about a quarter of body weight at the top dose over roughly a year, with individual results spread widely around that. Use the trial numbers as the reference, treat photos as anecdotes, and expect the full phase 3 program to refine the picture.
