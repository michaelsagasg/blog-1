Retatrutide is being tested for obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) in the phase 3 TRIUMPH program. Results have not been reported yet. The closest evidence is tirzepatide, which cut breathing pauses by up to about 63% in adults with obesity and OSA in the SURMOUNT-OSA trials, and is now approved for this use in the US.

## Why sleep apnea and weight go together

In OSA, the airway collapses repeatedly during sleep, causing breathing pauses and drops in oxygen. Excess weight, especially around the neck and upper airway, is a major cause. Weight loss reduces fat around the throat and improves airway stability. Studies show that each 10% drop in weight lowers the apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) by roughly 25% to 30% on average.

## What TRIUMPH tests

Retatrutide's phase 3 program includes a sleep apnea study within TRIUMPH-1 for adults with obesity and OSA. The main measure is the change in AHI, the number of breathing pauses per hour of sleep, from baseline to a set time point. Secondary measures usually include oxygen levels, sleepiness scores, and body weight.

Details, timing, and results are on the trial registry and will be reported in company releases and publications.

## The benchmark from tirzepatide

SURMOUNT-OSA tested tirzepatide in adults with moderate to severe OSA and obesity, with and without positive airway pressure treatment. Over 52 weeks:

- AHI fell by about 25 to 29 events per hour with tirzepatide compared with 5 to 6 with placebo.
- Relative reductions reached about 55% to 63%.
- A substantial share of participants had resolution or mild residual disease.

Retatrutide, with higher weight loss in other trials, could produce larger effects, but that is a hypothesis until the trial reports.

## Severity categories

| AHI (events per hour) | Category |
|---|---|
| Below 5 | Normal |
| 5 to 14 | Mild |
| 15 to 29 | Moderate |
| 30 or more | Severe |

A drop from 40 to 15 moves someone from severe to moderate, which matters for oxygen levels and cardiovascular risk, though many people still need CPAP.

## What OSA treatment involves today

- **CPAP or other airway pressure devices,** the standard treatment.
- **Oral appliances** that move the jaw forward.
- **Positional therapy,** avoiding sleeping on the back.
- **Surgery,** including hypoglossal nerve stimulation in selected people.
- **Weight loss,** including tirzepatide in eligible adults.

## Why this matters

Untreated OSA is linked to high blood pressure, atrial fibrillation, heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, and daytime sleepiness. Drugs that treat both weight and OSA could reduce these risks, and each result strengthens the case for approval in this group.

## What to watch for

- Whether AHI reductions exceed those for tirzepatide.
- How many participants no longer meet OSA criteria.
- Effects on blood pressure and oxygen levels.
- Whether benefits hold after stopping, as weight returns.
- Any safety signals in this population, given that retatrutide raises heart rate.

## What people with OSA should do now

If you snore, feel unrefreshed, or stop breathing at night, ask about a sleep study. Do not stop CPAP because you started weight loss. Reduced apnea should be confirmed with repeat testing before changing treatment. Tirzepatide is an approved option for adults with obesity and moderate to severe OSA, and a clinician can assess whether it fits.

## Availability

Retatrutide is investigational. Sleep apnea results are pending, and the drug is not approved for OSA or any other use.

## Questions people ask

### Can weight loss cure sleep apnea?

Sometimes. Large weight loss can reduce AHI enough that some people no longer meet criteria, especially in mild or moderate cases. Severe cases often improve but need continued treatment. Re-testing is the way to know.

### Do weight-loss drugs replace CPAP?

Not automatically. In SURMOUNT-OSA, many participants stayed on CPAP. A sleep physician should decide when to change therapy, based on a repeat sleep study.

### Can I take sedatives or alcohol at night?

Both can worsen apnea, and alcohol also worsens nausea on GLP-1 drugs. Avoid them close to bedtime.

### What about surgery and anesthesia?

People with OSA face higher anesthesia risk, and GLP-1 drugs slow stomach emptying. Tell your surgical team about both.

## Bottom line

TRIUMPH-1 will test retatrutide against sleep apnea, and tirzepatide has set a high bar. Until results arrive, do not treat apnea with unverified products, and keep CPAP unless a sleep physician advises otherwise.
