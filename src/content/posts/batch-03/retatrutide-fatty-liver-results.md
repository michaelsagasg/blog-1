In a phase 2 sub-study of people with fatty liver disease, retatrutide cut liver fat by large amounts. At the two highest doses, most participants reached normal liver fat levels, below 5%, by week 24. The result was published in Nature Medicine in 2024.

## Background

Fatty liver disease, now called metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), affects about a third of adults worldwide. Its more serious form, MASH, involves inflammation and scarring, and can lead to cirrhosis and liver cancer. Weight loss is the main treatment. Resmetirom and semaglutide are approved for MASH in some regions.

## The sub-study

Researchers took participants from the phase 2 obesity trial who had at least 10% liver fat at baseline, measured with MRI. Liver fat was measured again at 24 and 48 weeks.

## What it found

- Liver fat fell in a dose-related way.
- At 24 weeks, average relative reductions were large, with the highest doses cutting liver fat by roughly 80%.
- Most people on 8 mg and 12 mg reached liver fat below 5%, which counts as resolving fatty liver by imaging.
- Reductions continued to 48 weeks.
- Results tracked closely with weight loss, though some effect looked greater than weight loss alone would predict.

Numbers differ slightly by dose and time point, so read the paper for exact values.

## Why the glucagon receptor may help

Glucagon signaling stimulates fat breakdown in the liver. Combined with GLP-1 and GIP effects on weight and insulin, this could explain a stronger reduction in liver fat than seen with less potent drugs. The link is plausible but not proven in humans.

## Liver enzymes

Levels of ALT and AST, liver enzymes that rise with liver injury, improved in the retatrutide groups. Improvements in fat and enzymes suggest less liver stress, but the study did not measure scarring by biopsy.

## What this does not show

1. **Fibrosis.** Fat reduction is not the same as reversing scarring. Biopsy trials are needed.
2. **Clinical outcomes.** It does not show fewer liver events, transplants, or deaths.
3. **Long-term effect.** The data covers up to 48 weeks.
4. **Sample size.** The sub-study was small, with a few dozen people.

## Ongoing and planned work

Lilly has phase 3 studies in retatrutide for MASLD or MASH and related outcomes. Those will use more people and longer follow-up, and some will use biopsy.

## What it means for people with fatty liver

Weight loss of 10% or more improves fatty liver, and larger losses can improve inflammation and even fibrosis. Any drug producing large weight loss will help, and retatrutide looks especially strong. Approved options already exist, including semaglutide for MASH in adults with moderate to advanced fibrosis in some regions.

## Practical steps for liver health

- Aim for gradual weight loss and avoid crash diets, which can worsen liver inflammation.
- Limit alcohol.
- Exercise regularly, which reduces liver fat even without weight loss.
- Control blood sugar and cholesterol.
- Ask a clinician about liver tests such as FIB-4 and elastography.

## Questions

### Does it treat fatty liver directly?

It reduced liver fat markedly in a small trial, but it is not approved for that use.

### Can fatty liver come back?

Yes, if weight returns. Liver fat tends to rise again with regain.

## Availability

Retatrutide is investigational. Any effects on the liver come from supervised trials with a pharmaceutical product.

## How liver fat is measured

The sub-study used MRI proton density fat fraction, a scan that reports the percentage of the liver made of fat. Normal is below about 5%. It is more precise than ultrasound and does not need a biopsy. Biopsy remains the standard for scarring and inflammation, which is why later trials add it.

## How this compares with other drugs

Semaglutide and tirzepatide also reduce liver fat, with tirzepatide showing large reductions in the SYNERGY-NASH trial, where a majority of participants had resolution of MASH on biopsy without worsening fibrosis. Retatrutide's imaging results look at least as strong, and a biopsy-based phase 3 will test whether it matches that.

## Who might benefit most

People with obesity, type 2 diabetes, or high triglycerides often have fatty liver without knowing it. Blood tests for liver enzymes can miss it, since enzymes are often normal. Anyone with metabolic risk factors can ask about a fibrosis screening score.

## Bottom line

Retatrutide produced some of the largest liver fat reductions reported in early trials. Larger studies with biopsy data will show whether that translates into less liver damage.
