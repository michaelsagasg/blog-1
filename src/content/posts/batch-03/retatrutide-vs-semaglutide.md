Semaglutide 2.4 mg produced about 15% average weight loss at 68 weeks in the STEP 1 trial. Retatrutide 12 mg produced 24.2% at 48 weeks in phase 2 and 28.7% at 68 weeks in TRIUMPH-4. The gap is large, but the trials differ, and semaglutide is the only one approved.

## Mechanism

- **Semaglutide** activates the GLP-1 receptor. It lowers appetite, slows stomach emptying, and improves blood sugar.
- **Retatrutide** activates GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors. The extra targets add effects on insulin response, fat metabolism, and energy expenditure.

## Weight loss compared

| Drug and trial | Duration | Average weight loss |
|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide 2.4 mg, STEP 1 | 68 weeks | about 15% |
| Retatrutide 12 mg, phase 2 | 48 weeks | 24.2% |
| Retatrutide 12 mg, TRIUMPH-4 | 68 weeks | 28.7% |

The semaglutide trial included adults with obesity or overweight and one related condition. TRIUMPH-4 included adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis. Those groups differ, so treat the comparison as a rough guide.

## Evidence base

Semaglutide has far more evidence:

- Multiple STEP trials in different groups
- The SELECT trial, which showed about 20% fewer major cardiovascular events in people with heart disease and overweight or obesity
- Years of real-world use since approval in 2021 for weight loss
- Approved versions for type 2 diabetes since 2017

Retatrutide has a phase 2 program and one phase 3 readout. Its outcomes trials are still running.

## Side effects

Both cause GI effects. Semaglutide's label warns about pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, kidney injury from dehydration, and a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors from animal studies. Retatrutide has shown heart rate increases and skin sensation changes. Full comparisons of rates require a head-to-head trial.

## Dosing

Semaglutide 2.4 mg is injected weekly after a 16-week escalation. Retatrutide trials also used weekly dosing with step-ups every four weeks or so, reaching 9 mg or 12 mg.

## Cost and access

Semaglutide is approved, though supply and coverage vary. Retatrutide has no price and no legal supply. Some vendors sell unverified retatrutide online, which carries quality and safety risks.

## Who might still prefer semaglutide

- People who cannot wait for approval.
- People with heart disease, given the outcomes data.
- People who prefer a well-known safety profile.
- People who lose enough on semaglutide and do not need more.

## Who might want to watch retatrutide

- People who need very large weight loss.
- People who did not respond enough to semaglutide.
- People with conditions like knee osteoarthritis or fatty liver, where larger weight loss brings extra benefit.

## Questions

### Can I switch from semaglutide to retatrutide?

Not in a legitimate way today, as retatrutide is not available. If approved, switching guidance will come from labels and trials.

### Is retatrutide just stronger semaglutide?

It is a different molecule with more targets, not a higher dose. That is why side effects and effects differ.

## Differences in trial design worth knowing

STEP 1 gave semaglutide to adults with obesity or overweight with at least one related condition, without diabetes, for 68 weeks with a 16-week escalation. TRIUMPH-4 gave retatrutide for 68 weeks to people with obesity and knee osteoarthritis. Both had lifestyle counseling. The 68-week match is helpful, but the populations differ, and the headline retatrutide number comes from a sponsor press release, so a peer-reviewed paper may refine it.

## What each drug means for other conditions

- **Heart disease:** semaglutide has proven benefit in the SELECT trial. Retatrutide's outcomes data are pending.
- **Kidney disease:** semaglutide has evidence from the FLOW trial in diabetes and kidney disease. Retatrutide has kidney outcome trials underway.
- **Fatty liver:** semaglutide has evidence in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis. Retatrutide's phase 2 liver fat data are strong but early.
- **Knee osteoarthritis:** both have shown pain benefits, with semaglutide in STEP 9 and retatrutide in TRIUMPH-4.

## Which trial is closest to a real comparison

The most useful comparison would be a randomized trial giving one group retatrutide and the other semaglutide or tirzepatide, with the same population and schedule. Until one is reported, treat differences between the numbers as a signal of direction, not size. Even so, a gap of this scale is unlikely to vanish entirely under a fair comparison.

## Bottom line

Retatrutide looks roughly 60% to 90% larger in trial averages, but semaglutide is proven, available, and backed by heart outcome data. Wait for head-to-head results and full phase 3 safety.
