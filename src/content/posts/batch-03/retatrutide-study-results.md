Retatrutide study results come in two forms: peer-reviewed papers and company press releases. The papers include phase 2 in obesity (NEJM, 2023), phase 2 in type 2 diabetes (Lancet, 2023), and a liver fat sub-study. The press release covers the first phase 3 trial, TRIUMPH-4.

Knowing which is which changes how much weight you give each number.

## Peer-reviewed phase 2 papers

Papers are reviewed by outside experts before publication and include full methods, tables, and adverse events.

Key numbers from the obesity paper:

- 48-week trial, adults with obesity or overweight plus a related condition.
- 12 mg: 24.2% average weight loss.
- Placebo: 2.1%.
- Side effects were mostly gastrointestinal and dose-related.

The paper also reports how many people reached 5%, 10%, and 15% weight loss thresholds, which press releases often omit.

## Press releases

The TRIUMPH-4 announcement said 12 mg produced 28.7% loss at 68 weeks. Press releases are written by the sponsor, choose which numbers to highlight, and have not been independently reviewed.

That does not make them wrong. It means details are missing, such as how missing data was handled and what the full adverse event list looks like.

## What to check in any study

Use this checklist:

1. Who was enrolled? Obesity only, or diabetes too?
2. How long did it last?
3. What was the comparison group: placebo or another drug?
4. Was the result "efficacy estimand" or "treatment-regimen estimand"?
5. How many people stopped treatment, and why?

## Two ways to count results

Trials often report two versions of the same outcome:

| Method | What it counts |
|---|---|
| Treatment-regimen estimand | Everyone assigned to the drug, even if they stopped |
| Efficacy estimand | Results if everyone took the drug as directed |

The efficacy number is higher. The first is closer to real life. Sponsors often lead with the efficacy figure, so read the fine print.

## What sample size tells you

The phase 2 obesity trial enrolled 338 adults across all groups, which is small. With a few dozen people per dose group, averages can shift when a handful of people respond unusually well or poorly. Phase 3 trials enroll more than a thousand people, so their averages are steadier.

## Industry funding

Eli Lilly funded the retatrutide trials. Industry-funded trials are standard for drug development and follow strict regulatory rules, but independent analysis after publication is still valuable.

## What no study has shown yet

- Outcomes over many years.
- Rare side effects that show up only in large populations.
- Heart attack and stroke reduction.
- How long weight loss lasts after stopping.

## Where to find the primary sources

Search for the trial by name in the journal or on ClinicalTrials.gov, where each phase 3 study has a listing with its design and end dates. Reading the registry entry shows what the researchers said they would measure before results came in.

## Availability

Retatrutide is not approved. Study results describe an investigational drug used under trial conditions.

## Reading a results table

Most trial papers include a table that looks like this:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mean change | The average across the group |
| Placebo-adjusted | Drug result minus placebo result |
| 95% CI | The range where the true average likely falls |
| p-value | How likely the gap is due to chance |

The confidence interval matters more than the p-value. A narrow range means the average is well estimated. A wide range, common in small phase 2 trials, means the true value could be well above or below the reported figure.

## Surrogate and clinical outcomes

Weight, HbA1c, and liver fat are surrogate outcomes. They predict health benefits but are not the benefits themselves. The outcomes people care about are fewer heart attacks, fewer strokes, less kidney failure, and longer life. Semaglutide and tirzepatide have outcome trials showing such benefits. Retatrutide's outcome trials are still running.

## Red flags in secondhand summaries

When you see retatrutide results on social media or forums, watch for:

- Numbers with no trial name or dose.
- Phase 2 results described as if they were approval-level evidence.
- Mixing results from different doses or durations.
- Claims about "research peptides" citing trial data from the pharmaceutical product.

The last point matters most. Trial results apply to the drug made and tested by Lilly. They do not apply to a vial from an unknown supplier.

## Bottom line

Trust papers more than press releases, trust phase 3 more than phase 2, and check the trial population before applying any number to yourself. When the full TRIUMPH data is published, the missing detail will fill in.
