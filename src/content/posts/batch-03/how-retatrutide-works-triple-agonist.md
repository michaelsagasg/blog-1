Retatrutide is a single molecule that activates three hormone receptors: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. GLP-1 and GIP lower appetite and improve blood sugar, while glucagon raises energy expenditure and fat breakdown. It is injected once weekly.

Drug developers call this a triple agonist. "Agonist" means it switches a receptor on.

## The three receptors

### GLP-1

Glucagon-like peptide-1 is released from the gut after meals. Activating its receptor:

- Signals fullness to the brain
- Slows stomach emptying
- Raises insulin release when blood sugar is high
- Lowers glucagon when it is not needed

Semaglutide acts here.

### GIP

Glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide is another gut hormone. It boosts insulin release, may improve how fat tissue stores and uses energy, and may reduce nausea when combined with GLP-1. Tirzepatide acts on GIP and GLP-1.

### Glucagon

Glucagon is made in the pancreas and raises blood sugar by releasing glucose from the liver. It also increases energy expenditure and fat burning in the liver. Because it can raise blood sugar, it has been considered risky alone, but the GLP-1 and GIP actions balance it.

## Why combine them

The idea is that each receptor adds something:

| Receptor | Main contribution |
|---|---|
| GLP-1 | Appetite and blood sugar |
| GIP | Insulin response, possibly better tolerability |
| Glucagon | Energy expenditure and liver fat loss |

Retatrutide's design leans more on glucagon than earlier attempts, tuned to keep blood sugar in check.

## Why weekly dosing works

The molecule is a modified peptide with a fatty acid attached, which lets it bind to albumin in blood. That slows clearance, giving a half-life of about six days. One weekly injection keeps levels fairly steady.

## What the effects look like in trials

- Weight fell up to 24.2% at 48 weeks in phase 2 and 28.7% at 68 weeks in TRIUMPH-4.
- Blood sugar fell sharply in type 2 diabetes.
- Liver fat dropped, with most on higher doses reaching normal levels by 24 weeks.
- Heart rate rose in a dose-related way, likely tied to glucagon and GLP-1 actions.

## What is not fully known

- How much each receptor contributes to weight loss.
- Whether the energy expenditure effect is large in people.
- Long-term effects of glucagon activation on the heart and liver.
- Why some people respond much more than others.

## How it differs from older drugs

Older obesity drugs often acted on brain chemicals like serotonin or noradrenaline and carried cardiovascular and psychiatric risks. Gut-hormone drugs act on natural appetite pathways, which is part of why they work better and have a different safety profile.

## Availability

Retatrutide is investigational, and its mechanism has been studied mostly in Lilly-sponsored trials. Products sold as retatrutide online are not verified to match the trial drug.

## Is a triple agonist riskier?

More targets can mean more effects, wanted and unwanted. Glucagon activation raises heart rate and can, in theory, raise blood sugar and affect the liver. Phase 2 showed a rise in resting heart rate that peaked and fell. Blood sugar improved rather than worsened. Liver enzymes and liver fat fell. So far the safety profile looks like the GLP-1 class with a few additions, but larger trials are needed.

## Other triple agonists in development

Several companies are developing multi-target obesity drugs, including combinations of GLP-1 with other hormones such as amylin. The field is moving toward more targets and higher weight loss, but each new target needs its own safety data.

## Questions

### Does it bind the receptors equally?

No. Retatrutide is engineered with different potencies at each receptor. It is reported to be more potent at GIP than at GLP-1 and less potent at glucagon, tuned to balance benefits and risks.

### Does the brain or the gut do the work?

Both. GLP-1 receptors in the brain reduce appetite, and receptors in the gut and pancreas affect digestion and insulin. The exact split for retatrutide is not fully known.

## Bottom line

Retatrutide combines three gut and pancreas hormone signals into one weekly injection. The added glucagon action may explain why weight loss in trials exceeds what earlier drugs achieved.
