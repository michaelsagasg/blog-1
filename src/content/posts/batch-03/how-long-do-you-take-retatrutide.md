Retatrutide trials have run for 24 to 68 weeks so far, but no one knows the recommended duration in real use because the drug is not approved. For related drugs like semaglutide and tirzepatide, treatment is meant to be long term, because weight tends to return after stopping.

## How long trials lasted

- The phase 2 diabetes trial ran 24 weeks.
- The phase 2 obesity trial ran 48 weeks.
- TRIUMPH-4 ran 68 weeks.
- Other phase 3 trials run longer, with extension periods and an outcomes trial that will last years.

Trial length is a study design choice, not a treatment recommendation.

## What happens when the drug is stopped

The best data come from related drugs:

- In STEP 1 extension, people who stopped semaglutide after 68 weeks regained about two thirds of the lost weight within a year.
- In SURMOUNT-4, people who switched from tirzepatide to placebo after 36 weeks regained about 14% of their body weight over the next 52 weeks, while those who kept taking it lost a further 5.5%.

These results support the view that obesity is a chronic condition and the drug controls it while taken.

## Why weight returns

Appetite hormones, energy use, and habits shift as weight drops. Stopping the drug lets hunger signals rise again. It is the same reason blood pressure drugs stop working when you stop taking them. It is a physiological effect and not a failure of willpower.

## Common approaches to duration

Guidelines for approved obesity drugs suggest:

- Continue while it is working and tolerated.
- Review after 12 to 16 weeks at a maintenance dose. Many labels say to stop if a certain weight loss threshold is not reached.
- Consider maintenance dosing, meaning a lower dose after reaching goal weight.
- Reassess yearly, with weight, blood pressure, glucose, and lipids.

Retatrutide has no official rule, but these approaches are likely to apply.

## Could you use it for a set period?

Some people hope to use a drug until they reach a goal weight and then stop. Trial data on related drugs shows that this usually leads to regain unless lifestyle changes are strong. A short course can still improve health markers, but the benefit fades with the weight.

## Deciding on a plan

Questions worth answering with a clinician before starting any long-term weight drug:

1. What is the target weight or health goal?
2. Will we consider a lower maintenance dose?
3. What is the plan if side effects appear?
4. What is the plan if I need to stop for cost, supply, or pregnancy?
5. Which habits will I keep, such as strength training and protein intake?

## Costs and access

Long-term use means long-term cost. Approved obesity drugs cost hundreds of dollars a month in the US before insurance. Supply interruptions happen. Planning for the end, or for interruptions, is part of a realistic plan.

## Safety over time

The main gap for retatrutide is long-term safety. Trials of about a year cannot tell us about risks that occur rarely or after years. The phase 3 program and post-approval monitoring will add data.

## Availability

Retatrutide is investigational, with no approved duration or label. Treatment length in trials does not equal a recommendation.

## Maintenance dosing

Some approved drugs allow a lower dose for maintenance. Semaglutide and tirzepatide labels list maintenance doses lower than the highest dose, used when the higher dose is not tolerated or not needed. Trials have not clearly shown that lower maintenance doses hold weight as well as staying on the top dose, but many people move to a maintenance dose in practice.

For retatrutide, phase 3 groups at 9 mg and 12 mg give some information on this. The 9 mg group's slightly lower weight loss suggests that even the two highest doses differ in effect.

## Reasons people stop

- Cost or supply problems
- Side effects
- Reaching a goal weight
- Pregnancy plans
- Surgery, where fasting rules may apply
- Life changes, such as illness

Knowing these in advance lets you make a plan rather than a sudden stop.

## Stopping safely

There is no known withdrawal syndrome for GLP-1 drugs, so stopping is not dangerous in that sense. But appetite usually returns over weeks. Setting up meal structure, protein targets, and activity before stopping can soften the change. Some clinicians taper the dose rather than stopping abruptly.

## Bottom line

Trials lasted up to 68 weeks, and evidence from similar drugs points to long-term use for lasting results. Any real plan should be built with a clinician and include a strategy for stopping.
