Industry analyses put the phase 3 success rate at roughly 55% to 60%, meaning about that share of drugs entering phase 3 go on to a regulatory submission. Of those submitted, a large majority are eventually approved, so the overall chance from phase 3 start to approval is around 50%.

The figure varies by disease area and by how success is defined.

## The numbers in context

A commonly cited analysis of clinical development from 2011 to 2020 found these average transition rates:

| Stage | Chance of moving to the next stage |
|---|---|
| Phase 1 to phase 2 | about 52% |
| Phase 2 to phase 3 | about 29% |
| Phase 3 to submission | about 58% |
| Submission to approval | about 91% |

The likelihood of a drug entering phase 1 reaching approval was under 10% on average. Once a drug is in phase 3, the odds are much better.

## Why phase 3 fails

Reasons a phase 3 trial fails include:

- The drug works less well in a larger, more varied group than phase 2 suggested.
- A safety problem appears that small trials missed.
- The comparison group does better than expected.
- The trial was not designed well enough to show a real effect.
- Business decisions, such as cost or competition, halt development.

## Why some areas do better

Metabolic disease has had higher success rates than cancer or brain disorders, partly because targets like blood sugar and weight are easy to measure and predictable. The incretin drugs, semaglutide and tirzepatide, showed consistent results across trials. That track record raises confidence in related drugs, but not to certainty.

## Where retatrutide stands

Retatrutide has strong phase 2 results, a first positive phase 3 trial, and a mechanism related to approved drugs. Those are positive factors. Open items are the remaining phase 3 trials, long-term safety, and regulators' review.

## What "successful" means

A trial is successful if it meets its primary endpoint, such as a specified weight loss compared with placebo. A trial can meet its endpoint and still have concerns, such as high dropout or a side effect that limits use. Approval also depends on the benefit-risk balance, not only on hitting an endpoint.

## Approval after success

Even after a positive phase 3 program, regulators can:

1. Approve with a full label.
2. Approve with limits, such as a narrower population.
3. Ask for more data before deciding.
4. Reject the application.

Rejection after a successful phase 3 is uncommon but not unheard of.

## Reading news about phase 3 results

- "Met primary endpoint" means the main goal was reached.
- "Topline" means early, headline results.
- "Failed to meet" means the trial did not show a difference large enough.
- Look for safety details and subgroup information when available.

## What this means for people watching retatrutide

## Common questions

### Does a phase 3 success mean the drug is safe?

It means the drug met its main goals and no disqualifying safety problem appeared in the trial. Rare side effects, such as one in 10,000, can only be found after many more people take the drug, which is why regulators require post-approval monitoring.

### Do companies publish failed trials?

Not always promptly. Registries require results reporting, and journals are pushing for it, but some negative trials appear late or only in summary. That makes success rates slightly overstated in published literature.

### Are obesity drugs different?

They have had a good record recently. Semaglutide, tirzepatide, and other incretin-based drugs succeeded in phase 3 for weight loss. Earlier obesity drugs had a poorer record, with several withdrawn for safety reasons such as heart valve problems or psychiatric effects. That history is why regulators are cautious about safety.

### Does positive topline data change the odds?

Yes. Once a key phase 3 trial hits its primary endpoint with a strong effect, the chance of eventual approval rises well above the 55% to 60% average for the phase.

## Bottom line

About 55% to 60% of drugs succeed in phase 3, and most of those get approved. Retatrutide's early phase 3 data is positive, but the process is not complete.
