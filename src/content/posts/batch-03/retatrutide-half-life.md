Retatrutide has a half-life of about six days, which is why it can be given once a week. It takes roughly four to five weeks of steady dosing to reach stable levels, and about a month after the last dose for levels to fall to near zero.

## What half-life means

The half-life is the time it takes for the amount of drug in the blood to fall by half. If a dose starts at 100 units, after one half-life it is 50, after two it is 25, and so on.

With a six-day half-life:

| Days after dose | Fraction remaining |
|---|---|
| 6 | 50% |
| 12 | 25% |
| 18 | 12.5% |
| 24 | 6.25% |
| 30 | about 3% |

## Steady state

When you dose weekly, each new dose lands on top of what is left from earlier ones. Levels rise for the first several doses and then plateau. The plateau, called steady state, is reached after about four to five half-lives, which for this drug is about four to five weeks.

This explains two things:

1. **Slow start.** The first doses are lower than the later ones because drug accumulates.
2. **Effects of dose changes.** A dose increase takes about a month to show its full effect, which fits the four-week step schedule.

## Why the drug lasts so long

A natural GLP-1 lasts only minutes in the body because enzymes break it down quickly. Retatrutide is engineered with modifications, including a fatty acid chain, that let it bind to albumin in blood. Bound drug is protected from breakdown and kidney clearance and is released slowly. Semaglutide has a half-life of about a week, and tirzepatide about five days, from similar designs.

## What it means for missed doses

Because levels fall by half in six days, a missed dose lowers drug levels but does not eliminate them. Approved weekly drugs give windows of several days to take a late dose. Retatrutide has no approved missed-dose rule. Taking extra to catch up is unwise because of the long half-life and the risk of side effects.

## What it means for side effects

If side effects appear, they will not disappear quickly. A dose that is too high stays in the body for weeks. That is another reason to escalate slowly, and why stopping a dose does not bring immediate relief.

## Stopping the drug

Levels drop to about 3% within a month of the last dose. Appetite tends to return over that period. Weight regain follows over months.

## Pregnancy planning

Drug labels for long-acting GLP-1 drugs recommend stopping weeks before a planned pregnancy to allow clearance. Semaglutide's label suggests at least two months. Retatrutide has no pregnancy data, and a clinician should guide timing.

## Surgery and anesthesia

Slower stomach emptying raises the risk of food remaining in the stomach during anesthesia. Guidance for approved weekly drugs has been updated in recent years, with recommendations to hold or adjust based on risk. A person on any GLP-1 based drug should tell the anesthesia team.

## Variation between people

Body size, kidney function, and other factors affect drug levels. Larger people may have lower concentrations at the same dose. That is one reason effects and side effects vary at the same milligram dose.

## Availability

Retatrutide is investigational. The half-life comes from clinical pharmacology studies of the trial drug. Unverified products may not match its properties.

## Peak and trough

After each injection, levels rise over about one to three days, peak, and then fall slowly over the week. With a long half-life, the difference between peak and trough at steady state is modest, which helps keep appetite control steady and reduces the "wearing off" feeling some daily drugs have. Some people still notice appetite creeping back on days six and seven, especially at lower doses.

## Interactions with other medicines

Because retatrutide slows stomach emptying, the absorption of oral medicines can change, especially those with a narrow safety margin, such as thyroid hormone, some antiepileptics, and oral contraceptives. Tirzepatide's label recommends a backup contraceptive method for four weeks after starting and after each dose increase. Retatrutide has no label, but a pharmacist can advise on timing for any oral drug.

## Bottom line

A six-day half-life means weekly dosing, a month to reach full effect after any dose change, and about a month to clear. Slow escalation and caution with missed doses follow from that.
