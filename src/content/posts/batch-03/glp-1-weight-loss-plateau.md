A weight plateau is normal on GLP-1 drugs. In trials, weight fell fastest for the first 6 to 9 months and then leveled off, usually somewhere between 12 and 18 months. A plateau does not mean the drug stopped working. It means your body has reached a new balance.

## Why plateaus happen

- **A smaller body burns fewer calories.** Losing 20% of body weight lowers daily energy needs by a few hundred calories.
- **Metabolic adaptation.** The body reduces energy expenditure more than expected from weight loss alone.
- **Appetite adaptation.** Hunger hormones shift to push weight back up, and the drug offsets them only partly.
- **Habit drift.** Portions and snacks creep back over time.
- **Muscle loss.** Less muscle lowers energy use.
- **Dose limits.** Some people are already at their maximum tolerated dose.

## What the trials show

In semaglutide and tirzepatide trials, average weight curves flatten after about a year. In retatrutide phase 2, weight was still falling at 48 weeks at the top doses, and TRIUMPH-4 reached 28.7% at 68 weeks. The plateau, when it comes, is a broad flattening rather than a wall.

## How to tell a plateau from a slow week

A true plateau lasts four or more weeks with no downward trend in weekly averages, despite consistent habits. Shorter stalls come from water retention, salt, hormones, or constipation.

## Checklist before changing anything

1. **Weigh and measure consistently.** Same scale, same time, weekly average.
2. **Track intake for a week.** Portions drift, and liquid calories add up.
3. **Check protein.** Aim for about 1.2 to 1.6 g per kg body weight.
4. **Review activity.** Steps and strength training both often decline.
5. **Sleep and stress.** Poor sleep raises hunger and lowers willpower.
6. **Alcohol.** It adds calories and blunts fat loss.
7. **Medications.** Some raise weight, such as certain antidepressants, steroids, and insulin.
8. **Dose.** Confirm you are taking the dose you think you are, at a steady schedule.

## Options with a clinician

- Adjust the dose, if the drug allows a higher one and side effects are tolerable.
- Add resistance training or increase activity.
- Work with a dietitian for a structured plan.
- Review other conditions, such as thyroid disease or sleep apnea.
- Consider switching to a more potent drug when available.
- Accept the plateau as a maintenance phase if you reached a healthy range.

## Is a plateau a failure?

No. Maintaining a large weight loss is an achievement, since the body works to regain it. Health benefits depend on the weight you hold, not on whether the scale keeps falling.

## What not to do

- Do not cut calories dramatically.
- Do not raise the dose on your own.
- Do not add unverified drugs or stacks hoping to break the plateau.
- Do not weigh yourself several times a day.

## Muscle and a plateau

If weight is flat but waist is shrinking and strength is rising, you may be gaining muscle while losing fat. That is a good sign. Measurements beyond the scale show it.

## Availability

Retatrutide is investigational, so plateau patterns for it are mainly from trials. General principles here come from experience with approved GLP-1 drugs.

## Plateau versus stopping response

If weight rises steadily while you take the drug as usual, the cause may be changes in habits, medication, hormones, or health conditions, rather than a plateau. Pregnancy, thyroid changes, and new medicines are all possible causes. Rising weight with swelling or shortness of breath needs prompt medical care, since fluid retention can signal heart or kidney problems.

## Maintenance mindset

The goal after a large weight loss is to protect health and function. People who maintain weight loss often share habits: regular weighing, consistent meals, daily movement, strength training, and quick action when weight creeps up by a few pounds. These are more useful than searching for a new trick to restart weight loss.

## Long-term plateau data from trials

In SURMOUNT-1, tirzepatide weight curves flattened at around 60 to 72 weeks. In SURMOUNT-4, people who stayed on treatment after 36 weeks lost a further 5.5% over the next year, showing that loss can continue slowly beyond the first plateau.

## Bottom line

Plateaus are a normal feature of GLP-1 treatment. Check habits and measurements, involve a clinician, and treat maintenance as a success.
