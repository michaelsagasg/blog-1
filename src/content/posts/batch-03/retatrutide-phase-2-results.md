The phase 2 retatrutide trial in obesity found average weight loss of 24.2% at 48 weeks on the 12 mg dose. It was published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2023 and remains the most detailed public dataset on the drug.

## Trial design

- 338 adults with obesity, or overweight plus a weight-related condition, without diabetes.
- Randomized to weekly injections of placebo or retatrutide at 1, 4, 8, or 12 mg.
- 48 weeks of treatment, with a slower dose escalation in some groups.
- Primary outcome: percent change in body weight at 24 weeks. Secondary outcomes included week 48.

## Results by dose

| Group | Weight change at 48 weeks |
|---|---|
| Placebo | -2.1% |
| 1 mg | -8.7% |
| 4 mg | -17.1% |
| 8 mg | -22.8% |
| 12 mg | -24.2% |

At 24 weeks, the 12 mg group had lost roughly 17.5% on average, so a large share of the total came in the first half of the trial. The 48-week numbers are the ones most often quoted.

## Threshold results at 48 weeks

The paper also reported how many people crossed weight-loss thresholds. At the higher doses, nearly all participants lost at least 5%, most lost at least 15%, and a large share lost 25% or more. Those figures are far above what earlier approved drugs achieved, and the paper's tables give the exact percentages.

## Why slower dose escalation mattered

The trial compared a faster and slower titration for the 8 mg and 12 mg groups. The slower approach, starting at 2 mg and stepping up, had fewer early gastrointestinal side effects. Weight loss at 48 weeks was similar. That result shaped the dose plans used in phase 3.

## Safety findings

The most common side effects were:

- Nausea
- Diarrhea
- Vomiting
- Constipation

They were mostly mild or moderate and most common during dose increases. Discontinuations due to adverse events were higher at 8 and 12 mg than in the placebo group.

Other findings:

- Heart rate rose in a dose-related way, peaking at week 24 and then decreasing.
- Dysesthesia, an altered skin sensation, was reported more at the highest doses.
- Blood pressure fell across retatrutide groups.

## What it did beyond weight

Participants had improvements in waist circumference, blood pressure, and lipid levels. Markers of glucose control improved, including fasting glucose and insulin, even though participants did not have diabetes.

## Limitations

1. Small groups, around 50 people per dose.
2. Only 48 weeks of follow-up.
3. A population without diabetes, so it does not speak to that group.
4. Weight was still falling at 48 weeks in the high dose groups, so the final plateau is unknown.

## Why phase 2 matters for phase 3

Phase 2 is designed to find the dose range and spot problems before spending on a larger program. It picked the 9 mg and 12 mg range for phase 3 and showed that side effects could be managed with slow escalation. TRIUMPH-4 then confirmed and extended the result.

## Comparison with other drugs at the time

Phase 2 was not a head-to-head trial, so any comparison is indirect. For orientation, semaglutide 2.4 mg averaged about 15% weight loss in STEP 1 at 68 weeks, and tirzepatide 15 mg averaged about 21% in SURMOUNT-1 at 72 weeks. Retatrutide's 24.2% at 48 weeks was higher in a shorter time, which is why the drug drew attention.

Differences in populations, trial design, and how missing data is handled all affect these numbers, so treat the gap as suggestive.

## The mechanism behind the result

Retatrutide activates three receptors: GLP-1 and GIP, which tirzepatide also targets, plus the glucagon receptor. Glucagon signaling is thought to raise energy expenditure and burn liver fat. Phase 2 was the first large test of whether adding that third target would give more weight loss, and the dose-response curve suggested it did.

## What phase 2 did not tell us

The trial did not show whether the weight lost would stay off, whether heart rate changes matter long term, or how the drug performs in people with diabetes, heart disease, or kidney disease. Those questions were handed to later trials.

## Bottom line

Phase 2 established retatrutide as the most potent obesity drug tested at that time, with a familiar but dose-sensitive side effect pattern. It is a good foundation but one small study. Read it alongside the phase 3 data as it is released.
