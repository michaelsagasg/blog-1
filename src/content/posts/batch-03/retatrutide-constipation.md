Constipation was one of the most common gastrointestinal side effects in retatrutide trials, along with nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. It is a class effect of GLP-1 based drugs, caused by slower gut movement and lower food and fluid intake. Fluids, fiber, movement, and simple laxatives help most people.

## Why it happens

- **Slower gut transit.** GLP-1 drugs slow the movement of food through the stomach and intestines.
- **Eating less.** Less food means less bulk in the colon.
- **Drinking less.** Reduced thirst leads to harder stools.
- **Less fiber.** Smaller meals often skip vegetables and whole grains.
- **Less activity.** Fatigue or nausea reduces movement.
- **Other medicines.** Iron, calcium, some painkillers, and antacids can add to it.

Constipation often begins during dose increases and can come and go.

## What counts as constipation

- Fewer than three bowel movements per week
- Hard, dry, or lumpy stools
- Straining
- A feeling of incomplete emptying
- Bloating or belly discomfort

Everyone's normal differs. A change from your usual pattern matters more than a fixed number.

## Steps that help

### Fluids

Aim for pale yellow urine. A common target is about 2 to 3 liters of fluid a day unless a doctor limits fluids. Warm drinks in the morning can trigger a bowel movement.

### Fiber

Aim for about 25 to 35 g a day, building up slowly to avoid gas. Good sources:

| Food | Fiber (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Lentils, 1 cup cooked | 15 g |
| Raspberries, 1 cup | 8 g |
| Chia seeds, 1 tablespoon | 5 g |
| Oats, 1/2 cup dry | 4 g |
| Pear with skin | 5 g |
| Broccoli, 1 cup | 5 g |

Adding fiber without adding fluid can make constipation worse. Psyllium husk is a gentle option if taken with plenty of water.

### Movement

Walking for 20 to 30 minutes helps gut motility. Even short walks after meals make a difference.

### Routine

Try to use the toilet at the same time each day, often after breakfast, and do not ignore the urge.

## Over-the-counter options

- **Osmotic laxatives,** such as polyethylene glycol (Miralax), draw water into the stool and are commonly used long term.
- **Stool softeners,** such as docusate, are milder and less effective on their own.
- **Magnesium citrate or oxide** works for some, but should be used carefully in kidney disease.
- **Stimulant laxatives,** such as senna or bisacodyl, work well short term.
- **Fiber supplements,** such as psyllium.

Ask a clinician or pharmacist about the right choice, especially if you take other medicines.

## Prevention during dose increases

Start extra fluids and fiber the week before a dose step, and use a laxative early if you have gone two days without a bowel movement. Preventing a backlog is easier than clearing one.

## When to get help

- No bowel movement for several days with worsening pain or swelling
- Vomiting along with constipation and a swollen belly, which can suggest a blockage
- Blood in the stool
- Severe, persistent belly pain
- Constipation alternating with watery diarrhea, which can signal impaction with overflow

Labels for approved GLP-1 drugs list ileus and severe constipation as reported events, so do not ignore serious symptoms.

## Questions

### Can constipation cause weight plateaus?

It can hide fat loss for a few days by adding stool weight. Clearing it may show a sudden drop.

### Does it go away?

For many people it eases as the body adapts, though some have it as long as they take the drug.

## Availability

Retatrutide is investigational and has no label. The advice here follows general guidance for constipation on GLP-1 based drugs.

## Constipation, hydration and the drug's other effects

Constipation and dehydration feed each other. Vomiting or diarrhea on other days can also dry out stools. If you alternate between them, check fluid intake carefully and keep electrolytes balanced. Magnesium in a laxative can cause diarrhea, so dose it carefully.

## A simple daily routine

1. Warm drink and a fiber-rich breakfast, such as oats with fruit.
2. Ten-minute walk after meals.
3. A water bottle refilled three to four times a day.
4. A vegetable or legume at lunch and dinner.
5. A scheduled toilet break after breakfast.
6. A laxative kept on hand for days without a bowel movement.

Keep the plan flexible around dose days, when appetite and fluids often fall.

## Bottom line

Constipation is expected on retatrutide-type drugs. Fluids, fiber, movement, and an early laxative usually keep it under control, and severe symptoms need medical review.
