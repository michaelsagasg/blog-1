Phase 3 trials usually take one to four years from first enrollment to final data, and a whole phase 3 program can take longer. Obesity trials commonly treat people for 68 to 80 weeks, while cardiovascular outcomes trials can run for five years or more.

## What phase 3 does

Phase 3 confirms that a drug works and is safe in a large group. It typically enrolls hundreds to thousands of people, compares the drug with placebo or another treatment, and runs long enough to see the benefits and side effects that matter.

## Typical lengths by trial type

| Trial type | Typical treatment length |
|---|---|
| Diabetes glucose control | 26 to 52 weeks |
| Obesity weight loss | 68 to 80 weeks |
| Safety extension | 1 to 2 additional years |
| Cardiovascular outcomes | 3 to 6 years |

Retatrutide's TRIUMPH-4 lasted 68 weeks, in line with the obesity standard.

## Why it takes longer than treatment time

Total time includes:

1. **Setup.** Sites, regulators, and ethics boards approve the study, which can take months.
2. **Enrollment.** Recruiting thousands of participants can take 6 to 18 months.
3. **Treatment.** The core period, such as 68 weeks.
4. **Follow-up.** Additional weeks to check for safety after treatment ends.
5. **Data analysis.** Cleaning, locking, and analyzing the data takes a few months.
6. **Reporting.** Topline results come first, and full papers follow.

## Why obesity trials run about 1.5 years

Weight loss with these drugs keeps building for many months. Trials need enough time for weight to stabilize and for side effects to show up. Regulators also want enough people treated for a year or more for safety.

## The outcomes wrinkle

Heart attacks and strokes are relatively rare, so outcomes trials need many participants and years of follow-up to gather enough events. They often continue after a drug is approved. Semaglutide's SELECT trial ran about 3.3 years on average, with results in 2023, well after the drug was approved for weight loss.

## Phase 3 programs versus single trials

A program includes multiple trials with different populations. Retatrutide's TRIUMPH program starts in different years, so readouts arrive over time. The final application usually waits for the key trials, not all of them.

## What comes after

- Submission to regulators
- Review, typically 10 to 12 months for a standard US review of a new molecular entity
- Decision and label
- Launch and post-marketing monitoring

## How to estimate readout dates

Look up trials on ClinicalTrials.gov and read the "primary completion date," which is when the last participant's main data is collected. Add a few months for analysis. Dates often shift by months.

## Why phase 3 matters for participants

People in phase 3 usually receive study drug or placebo under monitoring. Some trials offer the active drug to placebo participants after the main period. Enrollment often closes long before results appear.

## Availability

Retatrutide is investigational. Trial timelines do not determine when, or whether, it will be available.

## Example: a hypothetical program timeline

This table shows how a program might unfold for a drug like retatrutide. Dates are illustrative only.

| Step | Approximate duration |
|---|---|
| Set up sites and start enrolling | 3 to 6 months |
| Enroll the full trial | 6 to 12 months |
| Treat participants | 68 to 80 weeks |
| Follow-up after treatment | 1 to 3 months |
| Analyze data and report topline | 2 to 4 months |

Adding these gives roughly three to four years from start to topline for a large obesity trial that begins enrolling from scratch, though overlapping steps shorten it. In practice, some participants finish while others are still enrolling.

## What participants experience

People in phase 3 usually attend visits every few weeks at the start and less often later. Visits include weight checks, blood tests, heart monitoring, and questionnaires. Participation typically lasts the full treatment period plus follow-up. Many trials provide counseling on diet and activity for everyone, including the placebo group.

## Why trials sometimes stop early

An independent monitoring board can stop a trial early for clear benefit, clear harm, or futility. Early stops are more common in outcomes trials. Obesity weight-loss trials tend to run to their planned end.

## Bottom line

Plan on one to four years for a phase 3 program. Obesity trials usually run about 68 to 80 weeks of treatment, and outcomes trials run much longer.
