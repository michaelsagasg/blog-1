Retatrutide is being tested in a large phase 3 program. TRIUMPH trials cover obesity and related conditions, and TRANSCEND trials cover type 2 diabetes. Only one phase 3 result, TRIUMPH-4, had been announced at the time of writing.

## Why so many trials

Regulators want evidence for each group a drug will be used in. Weight loss in someone with obesity is a different question from blood sugar control in type 2 diabetes, or from breathing during sleep in someone with sleep apnea. Each gets its own trial.

## The TRIUMPH obesity program

| Trial | Population | Status |
|---|---|---|
| TRIUMPH-1 | Adults with obesity or overweight, including a sleep apnea and knee osteoarthritis sub-study | Ongoing |
| TRIUMPH-2 | Obesity with type 2 diabetes | Ongoing |
| TRIUMPH-3 | Obesity with established cardiovascular disease | Ongoing |
| TRIUMPH-4 | Obesity with knee osteoarthritis | Topline reported |
| TRIUMPH-OUTCOMES | Cardiovascular and kidney outcomes | Ongoing |

Trial names and designs come from public registries and may change, so verify on ClinicalTrials.gov.

## The TRANSCEND type 2 diabetes program

TRANSCEND-T2D trials compare retatrutide with placebo and with active drugs such as semaglutide in people whose diabetes is not controlled. Primary outcomes are mostly HbA1c and weight.

## What TRIUMPH-4 has shown

The 12 mg group lost an average of 28.7% of body weight over 68 weeks, about 71 pounds. Knee pain fell substantially. Side effects followed the expected pattern, with more reports of altered skin sensation at higher doses.

## What each trial will answer

- **Sleep apnea:** does weight loss reduce breathing pauses enough to matter?
- **Cardiovascular disease:** is the drug safe for people with heart disease?
- **Type 2 diabetes:** how does it compare with existing treatments for blood sugar?
- **Outcomes trial:** does it reduce heart attacks, strokes, and kidney failure?

The last is the largest and takes the longest, because events must accumulate.

## How long the program runs

Phase 3 trials for weight loss often last 72 to 80 weeks plus follow-up. The outcomes trial can run several years. Lilly has said multiple readouts are expected across the coming period, but dates change, so watch company announcements.

## What a mixed result would look like

Trials do not always agree. A drug might work well for weight loss but show a safety signal in a specific group, such as people with existing heart disease. Regulators then approve for some uses and not others, or ask for more data. Watching the full program matters more than any single readout.

## How to keep up

- Search each trial on ClinicalTrials.gov for status and estimated completion.
- Follow Lilly investor and press releases for toplines.
- Look for journal publications and conference presentations, where full data appears.

## Availability

Retatrutide is not approved, and being in phase 3 does not guarantee approval. Trial participants receive a pharmaceutical-grade product under monitoring.

## Retatrutide, the other TRIUMPH and TRANSCEND readouts to watch

Some readouts will matter more than others for different readers:

- People with type 2 diabetes should watch TRIUMPH-2 and the TRANSCEND trials, since diabetes usually blunts weight loss.
- People with heart disease should watch TRIUMPH-3 and the outcomes trial.
- People with sleep apnea should watch the sleep apnea sub-study of TRIUMPH-1.

## How regulators use multiple trials

The FDA usually expects two adequate and well-controlled trials, or one large trial with confirmatory evidence. For obesity drugs, the agency also wants safety data on at least about 3,000 people treated for a year or more. Retatrutide's program is designed to exceed those thresholds.

## What "topline" means in company announcements

A topline release gives the primary endpoint results. It usually skips subgroups, secondary endpoints, and the full adverse event tables. When a trial reports topline, treat it as a headline and expect the numbers to be refined in the paper.

## Reasons a trial might disappoint

- Higher dropout than expected, which lowers the average.
- A safety signal such as liver, heart rhythm, or psychiatric events.
- A smaller effect in a group with diabetes.
- Results that are strong but not better than tirzepatide by a meaningful margin.

Any of these would change how the drug is positioned, even if it still works.

## Reading trial registry entries

On ClinicalTrials.gov, look for: enrollment target, primary outcome, arms and doses, estimated primary completion date, and status. Estimated dates shift, so use them as guides only.

## Bottom line

One phase 3 trial has reported and the rest are in progress. The program is designed to answer safety and effectiveness for several groups. Expect a flow of results over the next few years rather than one moment of clarity.
