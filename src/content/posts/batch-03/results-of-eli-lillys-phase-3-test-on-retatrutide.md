Eli Lilly's first phase 3 retatrutide trial, TRIUMPH-4, found that adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis lost an average of 28.7% of their body weight, about 71 pounds, over 68 weeks on the 12 mg dose. Knee pain also fell substantially compared with placebo.

## The summary

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Trial | TRIUMPH-4 |
| Population | Adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis |
| Duration | 68 weeks |
| Top dose | 12 mg once weekly |
| Average weight loss | 28.7% (about 71 lb) |
| Knee pain | Substantial improvement |
| Announced by | Eli Lilly, as a topline press release |

## Why the number stood out

It is the largest average weight loss reported in a phase 3 obesity trial. For context, semaglutide 2.4 mg reached about 15% in STEP 1 and tirzepatide 15 mg about 21% in SURMOUNT-1. Different trials cannot be compared directly, but the gap is large.

## The dose groups

The trial tested several doses, including 9 mg and 12 mg. Higher doses produced more weight loss, consistent with the phase 2 finding that results rise with dose.

## Safety

- Most adverse events were gastrointestinal, mild to moderate, and mostly during dose increases.
- A larger share of people on higher doses reported dysesthesia, an altered skin sensation.
- More participants stopped treatment at higher doses.
- Lilly said some discontinuations happened when people felt they had reached a weight where they did not want to lose more.

Full adverse event data has not been detailed beyond the release.

## What the release did not include

Topline announcements usually leave out:

- Full pain data and scoring methods
- Results by subgroup, such as sex and age
- Full lists of adverse events
- How missing data was handled
- Secondary endpoints

These will come with a conference presentation and a journal paper.

## What it means

The result confirms the phase 2 finding in a larger and longer trial and adds evidence that big weight loss reduces knee pain. It does not tell us about long-term safety or about people with other conditions, such as diabetes.

## What Lilly said next

Lilly said the remaining phase 3 trials would report over time, and it planned to present detailed results. The company has not been the only source of analysis, so independent commentary from academic groups will follow the publication.

## How to read the result responsibly

1. It is one trial in a specific population.
2. The weight loss is an average, with a wide spread.
3. It comes from a sponsor press release, not a peer-reviewed paper.
4. It applies to the trial drug, not to products sold online.

## Availability

Retatrutide remains investigational and is not approved or prescribed. Additional phase 3 trials are ongoing.

## How Lilly reports results

Eli Lilly usually announces topline data by press release, then presents at a medical meeting, such as the American Diabetes Association or the European Association for the Study of Diabetes, and publishes in a journal like the New England Journal of Medicine or The Lancet. Investors also see the data in quarterly earnings. Each step adds detail.

## Numbers you may see quoted

You may see slightly different figures for the same trial. Common reasons:

- Efficacy estimand versus treatment-regimen estimand, meaning whether participants who stopped are counted or not.
- Pounds versus percent, since pounds depend on starting weight.
- Different dose groups, such as 9 mg versus 12 mg.
- Time points, such as 48 weeks versus 68 weeks.

When comparing sources, check which version each uses.

## How it fits into Lilly's portfolio

Lilly already sells tirzepatide as Mounjaro and Zepbound. Retatrutide is a follow-up aimed at higher weight loss. The company is also developing other obesity drugs, including oral options. Companies often run several programs at once, so news about one does not replace the others.

## How TRIUMPH-4 compares with earlier readouts

Compared with the phase 2 obesity trial, TRIUMPH-4 ran 20 weeks longer, reached a higher average, and used a population with a specific condition. Its 9 mg and 12 mg dose groups line up with the top of the phase 2 range. If the full publication confirms the topline, it will strengthen the case that phase 2 results were not a fluke.

## Bottom line

Lilly's TRIUMPH-4 result is a very strong efficacy signal with a manageable but real side effect pattern. The next readouts and the full paper will show how well it holds up.
