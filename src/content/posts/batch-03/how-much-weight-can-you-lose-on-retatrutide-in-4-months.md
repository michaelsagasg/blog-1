In the phase 2 trial, people on the higher retatrutide doses lost roughly 10% to 15% of their body weight by around month four, on average. For a 240 lb person, that is about 24 to 36 pounds. Individual results varied widely.

No trial reported an exact week 16 figure for every group, so this range is an estimate drawn from the published curve between week 12 and week 24.

## The numbers behind the estimate

The phase 2 obesity trial gave these anchor points for the 12 mg group: about 17.5% loss at 24 weeks and 24.2% at 48 weeks. The curve is steepest early, so by week 16 the average sat somewhere between the low teens and the mid-teens.

| Body weight at start | 10% loss | 15% loss |
|---|---|---|
| 180 lb | 18 lb | 27 lb |
| 220 lb | 22 lb | 33 lb |
| 260 lb | 26 lb | 39 lb |
| 300 lb | 30 lb | 45 lb |

Lower dose groups lost less. The 4 mg group ended at 17.1% after 48 weeks, so at four months it would have been well below the higher groups.

## Why the dose reached by month four matters

Trials raised the dose in steps every four weeks. By week 16, people on the slow schedule were typically on their fourth step, still below the final target for the 12 mg arm. Full effect comes later. That is why the four-month figure is a fraction of the 48-week result.

## What drives the spread between people

- **Starting weight.** Larger bodies lose more pounds for the same percentage.
- **Dose tolerated.** People who paused or reduced because of side effects lost less.
- **Sex and age.** Women often lose a slightly higher percentage, and older adults sometimes lose less.
- **Diabetes.** Loss is smaller in type 2 diabetes. The diabetes trial reported about 17% at 24 weeks for the top dose.
- **Adherence.** Missed doses lower drug levels.
- **Food intake.** The drug reduces appetite, but eating patterns still matter, especially protein.

## What about pace?

A loss of 10% to 15% in four months works out to about 0.6 to 0.9 lb per day of pace on average for a 240 lb person, or 1.5 to 2.5 lb per week. Faster loss raises the risk of muscle loss, gallstones, and hair shedding. Slower steady loss is easier on the body.

## What that weight is made of

Trial body composition data showed most weight lost was fat, with a smaller share lean mass. With rapid loss, lean mass share can rise. Resistance training and protein reduce the muscle loss. A DEXA scan is the best way to check.

## Should you expect this in real life?

Trial participants got regular visits, counseling, and a pharmaceutical product with a known dose. Real-world results for other drugs in this class are usually somewhat smaller than trial results, because people skip doses, stop early, or cannot reach the top dose. Retatrutide has no real-world data because it is not approved.

## A sensible way to think about four months

- Under 5%: common at low doses, not a failure.
- 5% to 10%: solid response.
- 10% to 15%: strong response and near the trial average at higher doses.
- Above 15%: on the higher end, and worth watching for muscle loss and dehydration.

## Availability

Retatrutide is not approved and is not prescribed. Anyone using an unverified product cannot know the dose, purity, or sterility, so trial results do not carry over.

## Questions people ask

### Is 30 pounds in four months realistic?

For someone starting near 250 lb or more, 30 pounds is about 12%, which is within the trial range at higher doses. For someone starting at 180 lb, it would be about 17%, which is above the four-month average. The percentage is what to compare, not the pounds.

### What if the scale stalls for two weeks?

Stalls are normal. Water retention, salt intake, hormones, and constipation can mask fat loss for a week or two. Look at waist size and the four-week trend instead of daily numbers.

### Does the loss slow down?

Yes. Both the trial curves and the experience with similar drugs show fast early loss and a slower pace later as the body reaches a new balance.

## Bottom line

Around 10% to 15% at four months is a reasonable trial-based estimate for higher doses, with a wide range. The bigger result comes after a year, and the exact number depends on the dose reached and how the body responds.
