Weekly GLP-1 type drugs are injected into the fat layer under the skin, called a subcutaneous injection. The standard sites are the abdomen, the front or outer thigh, and the back of the upper arm. Rotating the site each week reduces skin problems.

Retatrutide was given this way in trials. It is investigational and has no approved label, so this post uses the guidance from semaglutide and tirzepatide as the model.

## The three standard sites

| Site | Notes |
|---|---|
| Abdomen | At least 2 inches (5 cm) away from the navel. Most common site. |
| Thigh | Front or outer area, midway between hip and knee. |
| Upper arm | Back of the arm, easier if someone else injects. |

Semaglutide and tirzepatide labels list these same three sites and say any can be used with the same effect.

## Why rotate sites

Repeated injections in the same spot can cause lipohypertrophy, a lump of thickened fat tissue. Absorption from these areas can be uneven. Rotating also reduces bruising and irritation.

A simple pattern: left abdomen, right abdomen, left thigh, right thigh, then repeat. Keep each new spot about an inch from the last.

## Places to avoid

- Skin that is bruised, red, scarred, hard, or broken.
- Areas near the navel, ribs, or a recent surgery.
- Directly over a vein or muscle.
- Through clothing.

## Basic technique

1. Wash hands and check the product label and any liquid for particles or cloudiness.
2. Clean the site with an alcohol swab and let it dry.
3. Pinch the skin gently if you have little fat there.
4. Insert the needle at the angle the syringe or pen instructs, often 90 degrees for short needles.
5. Push the plunger slowly, wait a few seconds, then withdraw.
6. Dispose of the needle in a sharps container.

Never reuse or share needles.

## Does the site affect results?

For related drugs, absorption is similar across the abdomen, thigh, and upper arm. Some studies find slight differences in speed but not in overall effect. Injecting into muscle rather than fat can speed absorption unpredictably, so aim for the fat layer, especially in lean people.

## Reducing pain and reactions

- Let refrigerated product warm for a few minutes if instructions allow.
- Use a fresh, thin needle.
- Let the alcohol dry fully before injecting.
- Do not rub hard after the injection.

Mild redness or a small bump is common. Spreading redness, warmth, swelling, or pus can signal infection and needs medical attention. Hives, facial swelling, or trouble breathing is an emergency.

## Special situations

- **Lean individuals:** use a shorter needle and pinch the skin to avoid muscle.
- **Scar tissue or stretch marks:** avoid them.
- **Bruising:** press gently after and avoid injecting into visible veins.

## Sterility and product quality

Injection site infections often trace back to non-sterile technique or contaminated products. Peptides mixed at home from powder need sterile supplies and clean handling. Unverified vials may carry a contamination risk that a pharmaceutical pen does not.

## Availability

Retatrutide is not approved, and no official injection instructions exist. Talk with a clinician or pharmacist about technique for any injectable.

## Site-by-site comfort and practicality

Abdomen injections are the easiest to reach and see, and most people find them least painful because of the fat layer. Thigh injections sit over more muscle, so a short needle and a slight pinch help. Upper arm injections are hard to do on yourself, since you cannot see the site well and it can be tricky to pinch. A partner or caregiver can help with the arm.

## Needle length and gauge

Pens for approved drugs use very short, fine needles, often 4 to 5 mm and 30 to 32 gauge. Insulin syringes are similar. Longer needles increase the chance of reaching muscle, which can change absorption and hurt more. If someone is very lean, a short needle and pinch technique matter even more.

## Skin care and follow-up

- Check each site the next day for lumps or heat.
- If a lump forms, avoid that area for several weeks.
- Report persistent nodules to a clinician.

## Injection anxiety

Needle fear is common. Looking away, cooling the skin with ice for a minute, slow breathing, and injecting while seated can all help. Autoinjector pens hide the needle, which some prefer. Vials and syringes give more control but need more steps.

## Bottom line

Abdomen, thigh, and upper arm are the standard sites, and rotating weekly is the main rule. Use sterile technique, avoid damaged skin, and stay consistent.
