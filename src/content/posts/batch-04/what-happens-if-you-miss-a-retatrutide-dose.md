Retatrutide has no approved or official missed-dose guidance, since it remains an investigational drug without a medicines licence anywhere. What can be said comes from its pharmacokinetics, a half-life of roughly six days, and by looking at how approved GLP-1 based drugs with similar half-lives handle a missed weekly dose in their official guidance.

## What a six-day half-life implies about a missed dose

Because retatrutide clears slowly, missing a single weekly dose does not eliminate the drug from the body immediately. Roughly six days after the last dose, around half the previous level remains; by the time a full week has passed, levels have fallen further but have not reached zero. This means a short delay changes levels gradually rather than abruptly.

## How approved drugs with similar half-lives handle this

Tirzepatide's UK label for Mounjaro, and similar guidance for other approved weekly GLP-1 drugs, generally allows a missed dose to be taken within a defined window, commonly up to four days after the missed date, after which the missed dose is typically skipped and the regular schedule resumed at the next planned date rather than doubling up. This guidance exists because these are licensed medicines with formal prescribing information; retatrutide, without a licence, has no equivalent official instruction.

## Why doubling up is generally discouraged

Because retatrutide accumulates over time with weekly dosing, taking an extra dose to "catch up" would push levels higher than intended for that point in a schedule, increasing the likelihood of side effects like nausea without a established benefit. This reasoning follows from general pharmacology principles around a long half-life drug, not specific retatrutide trial data on missed-dose management.

## What trial protocols did

Clinical trial protocols for drugs like retatrutide typically define a visit window around each scheduled dose and record any deviation, but this operates under clinical trial monitoring and record-keeping, not as general public guidance for managing a missed dose outside that structured setting.

## Why this matters more for an unlicensed product

Because retatrutide has no approved label, there is no regulator-reviewed patient information leaflet addressing missed doses the way there is for a medicine like Mounjaro. Anyone using retatrutide who has questions about a missed dose is working from the same general pharmacology principles discussed here, and a healthcare professional is better placed to advise on individual circumstances than a general article.

## What research does support

The underlying principle, that missing part of a dosing interval changes levels gradually rather than abruptly given a multi-day half-life, and that catching up with an extra dose risks pushing levels higher than intended, both follow from well-established pharmacokinetic science, even though retatrutide-specific missed-dose research has not been published.

## What this article is not

This is a summary of the pharmacology involved, not personalised guidance on what to do if a dose is missed. Retatrutide remains investigational, and questions about a specific situation are best directed to a healthcare professional.

## Bottom line

Retatrutide has no official missed-dose guidance because it is not an approved medicine. Its roughly six-day half-life means levels fall gradually rather than dropping to zero after a missed dose, and general pharmacology principles suggest against trying to "catch up" with an extra dose, consistent with how approved drugs with similar half-lives are officially handled.
