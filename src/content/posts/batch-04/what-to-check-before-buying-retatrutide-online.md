Before buying retatrutide online, check five things on the listing: a batch-specific certificate of analysis, the seller's UK company details, the price per mg, the delivery and storage terms, and the returns policy. Doing this before you pay filters out most of the risk in a market with no pharmacy oversight.

## 1. Is there a batch-specific COA?

A certificate of analysis (COA) reports what a lab found when testing a specific batch, typically identity, purity and concentration. It should be visible on the product page, and the batch number on the report should match the one printed on your vial label. A generic COA, or none at all, is the single biggest warning sign on a listing.

## 2. Who is actually selling it?

Look for a UK business name, an address or company number, and at least two ways to contact them, such as email and a messaging app or phone line. An anonymous storefront with only a checkout page is much harder to hold accountable if the order does not match what was promised.

## 3. What does it actually cost per mg?

Vial sizes vary between sellers, so compare the price divided by the mg in the vial, not the headline price. A 10 mg vial at £45 is £4.50 per mg. A 15 mg vial at £58 is about £3.87 per mg. Once you have this figure, price comparisons between different sellers become meaningful.

## 4. How will it be delivered and stored in transit?

Retatrutide typically needs refrigeration, so check that the listing mentions insulated packaging or a cold pack, and states a dispatch cutoff and expected delivery window. A seller offering next-day tracked delivery with cold-chain packaging is giving you more assurance than one that says nothing about either.

## 5. What happens if something goes wrong?

A written returns and refund policy, covering damaged, late or incorrect parcels, gives you something to point back to if a problem arises. No written policy usually means no real recourse.

## Red flags that override an otherwise decent listing

- A COA that is not tied to a specific batch number
- Pricing dramatically below every comparable UK seller
- Only irreversible payment methods, with no card option
- Reviews that look templated or arrived in a short cluster

## A worked example

Say you find two listings for a 10 mg vial: one at £38 with no COA visible and no UK company name, and one at £46 from a seller like Enzo Meds with a batch-linked COA and clear delivery terms. The second costs £0.80 more per mg but gives you a way to verify what you are actually getting. That gap is usually worth paying for.

## What you are buying

Retatrutide remains an investigational drug, still in Phase 3 trials and without a UK medicines licence. In Eli Lilly's Phase 2 trial, the highest dose group lost an average of 24.2% of body weight at 48 weeks. Buying it online means buying a research-grade product with none of the built-in oversight of a licensed pharmacy dispensing, which is exactly why these five checks matter as much as the price.

## Bottom line

Run every retatrutide listing through the same five checks before you order: COA, seller identity, price per mg, delivery and storage terms, and returns policy. A listing that answers all five clearly is a safer choice than one that wins only on price.
