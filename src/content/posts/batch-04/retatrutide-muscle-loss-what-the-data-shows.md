Body composition data from GLP-1 and multi-agonist drug trials, including sub-studies involving retatrutide, has generally shown that some of the weight lost comes from lean mass, which includes muscle, alongside fat mass, a pattern that is not unique to retatrutide and has been observed across this class of drugs.

## What "lean mass" includes

Lean mass is a broader category than muscle alone. It includes skeletal muscle, but also organs, bone, water and other non-fat tissue. Body composition studies using methods like DEXA scanning report changes in lean mass as a category, which is not the same as measuring muscle strength or function directly.

## What has been reported across this drug class

Sub-studies of tirzepatide's SURMOUNT trials using body composition scanning have reported that a portion of total weight lost, commonly cited in a range of roughly one quarter to one third depending on the specific study, came from lean mass rather than fat mass. Retatrutide's own published body composition data is more limited so far, given its earlier stage in the trial process, but researchers have flagged similar patterns as an area of interest.

## Why weight loss generally includes some lean mass, regardless of method

This pattern is not unique to GLP-1 based drugs. Any method of significant weight loss, whether through calorie restriction, surgery or medication, typically results in some proportion of lean mass loss alongside fat mass loss, a well-established finding in weight-loss research generally. The proportion varies depending on factors including the rate of loss, dietary protein intake and physical activity.

## What may help preserve lean mass, based on broader research

Resistance exercise and adequate dietary protein intake are both associated with better preservation of lean mass during periods of weight loss in general population research, independent of which weight-loss method is used. This is general nutrition and exercise science, not retatrutide-specific trial data, but it is relevant background for anyone thinking about this question.

## What is not yet known specifically for retatrutide

Because retatrutide remains in Phase 3 trials, long-term body composition data specific to this drug, including how lean mass changes are affected by dose, treatment duration, or individual factors like age and baseline muscle mass, is more limited than for tirzepatide, which has a longer track record of published sub-studies.

## Why this is being actively studied

Given the strong weight-loss results reported in trials, researchers and clinicians have raised muscle preservation as an important area of ongoing study across this drug class, since substantial lean mass loss could carry its own health implications, particularly for older adults or anyone with lower baseline muscle mass. This remains an active area of research rather than a settled question.

## What this means for interpreting weight-loss headlines

A percentage of body weight lost in a trial does not distinguish between fat and lean mass unless the study specifically measured body composition. Headline weight-loss figures, including retatrutide's reported 24.2% at 48 weeks in its Phase 2 trial, describe total weight change, not the breakdown between fat and lean tissue.

## What this article does not provide

This summarises what body composition research across this drug class has reported. It is not guidance on diet, exercise or dosing for muscle preservation, which depends on individual circumstances and is best discussed with a healthcare professional.

## Bottom line

Weight loss involving GLP-1 based drugs, including what limited data exists for retatrutide, generally includes some proportion of lean mass alongside fat mass, consistent with weight loss more broadly. This remains an active area of research, particularly as retatrutide's own trial programme continues, and general factors like resistance exercise and protein intake are associated with better lean mass preservation in the wider research literature.
