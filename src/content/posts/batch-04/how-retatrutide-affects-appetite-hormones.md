Retatrutide works by activating three hormone receptors involved in appetite and metabolism: GLP-1, GIP and glucagon. Each plays a different role in how the body signals fullness, processes food and uses energy, and retatrutide's combined action on all three is what sets it apart from single or dual-receptor drugs.

## GLP-1: the fullness signal

GLP-1, or glucagon-like peptide-1, is a hormone released by the gut after eating. It slows how quickly the stomach empties, which extends the feeling of fullness after a meal, and it signals to the brain's appetite centres to reduce hunger. It also increases insulin release in response to rising blood sugar. This is the same receptor targeted by semaglutide and, alongside GIP, by tirzepatide.

## GIP: metabolic support alongside GLP-1

GIP, or glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide, is another gut hormone released after eating. It works alongside GLP-1 to support insulin release and has effects on fat tissue. Research suggests activating GIP alongside GLP-1 may enhance the overall metabolic effect compared with targeting GLP-1 alone, which is part of the basis for dual-agonist drugs like tirzepatide.

## Glucagon: the receptor that sets retatrutide apart

Glucagon receptor activity is usually associated with raising blood sugar by prompting the liver to release stored glucose, which sounds counterintuitive for a weight-related drug. However, glucagon receptor activation also increases energy expenditure, meaning the body burns through more energy at rest. In retatrutide, this glucagon effect is combined with GLP-1's appetite suppression and insulin support, aiming to balance out the blood sugar effect while adding an energy-expenditure component that GLP-1 and GIP alone do not provide.

## Why triple activation is the focus of research interest

The idea behind combining all three receptors is that each contributes a different mechanism: GLP-1 and GIP reduce appetite and support insulin regulation, while glucagon receptor activity adds an energy expenditure effect. Eli Lilly's Phase 2 trial reported an average weight loss of 24.2% at the highest dose after 48 weeks, higher than earlier trial results for GLP-1 alone or GLP-1 plus GIP over similar periods, which researchers have attributed in part to this added mechanism.

## What "affecting appetite hormones" does not mean

Activating these receptors changes signalling around hunger and fullness, but it is not the same as a guaranteed outcome for any individual, and trial averages describe a study population over a defined period, not a promise for every person who takes it. Response varies between individuals based on numerous factors.

## What retatrutide's regulatory status is

Retatrutide remains an investigational drug, still in Phase 3 trials, with no approval anywhere in the world. Its hormone-receptor activity has been studied in Eli Lilly's published trial data, but it is not yet a licensed medicine, and its long-term effects continue to be researched.

## Bottom line

Retatrutide's effect on appetite comes from activating three separate hormone pathways, GLP-1 for fullness and insulin support, GIP for additional metabolic effect, and glucagon for increased energy expenditure. This combination is the basis for the interest in its trial results, though it remains investigational and outcomes vary between individuals.
