Buying tirzepatide in the UK splits into two routes: a licensed prescription pen sold as Mounjaro through registered pharmacies, or a research-grade peptide vial from a specialist online seller. This guide covers both, along with the vial sizes, price per mg, COA checks and delivery details that matter for the peptide-seller route.

## What tirzepatide does

Tirzepatide activates the GLP-1 and GIP receptors. In Eli Lilly's SURMOUNT-1 trial, the highest dose group lost an average of around 21% of body weight over 72 weeks. As Mounjaro, it holds a UK licence for weight management and type 2 diabetes in eligible adults after a prescriber's assessment.

## The prescription route

Mounjaro is dispensed through a registered pharmacy following an assessment, either in person or via an online prescribing service. This route includes the oversight and manufacturing standards that come with a licensed medicine, and pricing typically bundles the consultation with the product.

## The peptide-seller route

Outside the prescription pathway, tirzepatide is sold as a research-grade peptide, usually in multi-dose vials. This is the market most comparisons of UK sellers refer to, and it carries none of the prescriber oversight of the licensed route, which shifts the responsibility for verification onto the seller's own documentation.

## Vial sizes

Peptide sellers commonly list tirzepatide from around 10 mg up to 60 mg vials. Larger vials generally carry a lower cost per mg. See [tirzepatide vial sizes explained](/posts/batch-04/tirzepatide-vial-sizes-explained/) for a full breakdown.

## Working out price per mg

A 30 mg vial at £140 is about £4.67 per mg. A 60 mg vial at £220 is about £3.67 per mg. Compare this figure across sellers rather than relying on the sticker price alone.

## Checking the COA

A trustworthy listing shows a certificate of analysis on the product page with a batch number matching your vial. Enzo Meds is one example of a UK seller publishing a COA per batch. See [why a COA matters when buying peptides](/posts/batch-04/why-a-coa-matters-when-buying-peptides/) for what to look for in the report.

## Company transparency

A real UK business name, address or company number, and more than one way to make contact, are basic signs a seller can be held accountable if something goes wrong.

## Delivery expectations

Royal Mail Tracked 24 or a courier with next-day delivery is standard, and cold-chain packaging matters since tirzepatide typically needs refrigeration. A listing with no mention of this for a product needing cold storage is worth a direct question first.

## Payment methods

Card payment through a processor is most common, alongside bank transfer and sometimes cryptocurrency. Card payment generally offers a clearer route to a refund through your bank.

## Returns policy

Look for written terms on damaged, late or incorrect orders. No written policy means little recourse if something goes wrong.

## Red flags

- No COA, or one without a matching batch number
- No UK company details
- Pricing well below every comparable listing
- Vague or missing delivery information

Score a shortlist of UK sellers on COA transparency, price per mg, delivery terms and company details. See [how to compare tirzepatide sellers in the UK](/posts/batch-04/how-to-compare-tirzepatide-sellers-uk/) for a step-by-step method.

## Bottom line

Decide first whether you want the licensed prescription route or the research-grade peptide market, then apply the same checklist to any seller you consider: COA, price per mg, company transparency, delivery terms and returns policy.
