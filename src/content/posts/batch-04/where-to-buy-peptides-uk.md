Most peptides sold in the UK, including retatrutide and research versions of tirzepatide, come from specialist online sellers rather than pharmacies. Pharmacies only stock the small number of peptides that hold a UK licence, such as Mounjaro. Everything outside that list is sold through a separate market of online retailers, and the checklist for choosing one is different from choosing a pharmacy.

## The two UK peptide markets

**Licensed pharmacy peptides.** A short list of peptide-based medicines, like tirzepatide as Mounjaro, are licensed for specific medical uses and dispensed through registered pharmacies with a prescription.

**Research-grade peptide sellers.** Everything else, from retatrutide to a long list of other peptides, is sold by online businesses outside the pharmacy system, usually labelled for research purposes. This is the market most people mean when they search for where to buy peptides in the UK.

## What a trustworthy listing shows

- **A certificate of analysis (COA) per batch.** The batch number on the report should match the one on the vial label.
- **Clear vial size and concentration**, so you can calculate price per mg.
- **A real UK business identity.** A company name, an address or a company number, not just a contact form.
- **Written delivery and returns terms.** Look for this on the site itself, not only in an email reply after you ask.

Enzo Meds is one example of a listing structured this way, with a batch-linked COA on its product pages. Treat it as a benchmark for the kind of detail any seller should provide, not as the only option worth considering.

## Typical price ranges

Prices vary a lot by peptide and vial size, but as a general pattern, expect somewhere between £2 and £6 per mg for common GLP-1 style peptides in the UK market. Smaller vials tend to cost more per mg than larger ones because packaging, shipping and testing costs are fixed regardless of size.

## Delivery in the UK

Royal Mail Tracked 24 and courier services are the most common delivery methods, with next-day arrival typical for orders placed before an early afternoon cutoff. Peptides generally need cold storage, so check that the seller mentions insulated packaging or a cold pack, especially in warmer months.

## Payment methods

Card payment through a processor is standard, and many sellers also accept bank transfer. Some accept cryptocurrency. Paying by card generally gives you a clearer path to a refund through your bank if an order goes wrong.

## Red flags to watch for

- No COA anywhere on the listing, or one that is not batch-specific
- Prices dramatically below every comparable seller
- No UK company details listed
- Stock photography instead of real product images
- A wall of near-identical five-star reviews posted in a short window

## How to compare sellers

Line up two or three sellers side by side on the same four criteria: COA transparency, price per mg, delivery terms and company details. A seller that is strong on all four is a safer choice than one that only wins on price. ## Why the COA matters so much

A certificate of analysis states what a lab found when it tested a specific batch: usually identity, purity and concentration. Without one, there is no independent check on what is actually in the vial. With one tied to the batch number on your product, you can at least confirm the seller is testing what they sell, rather than taking the label on trust. Our guide on [why a COA matters when buying peptides](/posts/batch-04/why-a-coa-matters-when-buying-peptides/) goes through how to read one.

## Starting small

If you are new to buying peptides in the UK, ordering the smallest available vial size from a seller first is a reasonable way to check delivery speed, packaging quality and COA accuracy before committing to a larger order.

## Bottom line

Peptide buying in the UK runs almost entirely through specialist online sellers rather than pharmacies. Compare listings on COA transparency, price per mg, delivery terms and company details before you order, and use a well-documented listing like Enzo Meds as your reference point for what good practice looks like.
