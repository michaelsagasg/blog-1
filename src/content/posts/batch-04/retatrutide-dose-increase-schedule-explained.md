Eli Lilly's Phase 2 retatrutide trial used a staged dose-escalation schedule, starting participants at a low weekly dose and increasing roughly every four weeks up to their assigned target dose. This step-up approach is standard practice across GLP-1 based drug trials and is designed to assess tolerability as the dose rises, rather than starting everyone at a high dose immediately.

## Why trials escalate dose gradually

Starting at a higher dose immediately tends to increase the likelihood and severity of gastrointestinal side effects like nausea, which are common with GLP-1 based drugs, especially early in treatment. A gradual increase allows the body more time to adjust at each step, which trial data across this drug class has generally shown reduces the rate of side effects severe enough to cause discontinuation.

## The general structure used in Phase 2 trials

Participants began at a low starting dose and moved up in defined increments approximately every four weeks, until reaching their assigned target maintenance dose, which varied by study arm since different participant groups were assigned different final target doses to compare outcomes.

## Why four weeks between steps

Because retatrutide has a half-life of around six days, it takes roughly four to five weeks of consistent dosing to reach a stable, steady level in the blood at any given dose. Waiting about four weeks between increases allows levels to largely stabilise at each step before moving to the next, which is consistent with the pharmacokinetics of the drug rather than an arbitrary interval.

## What the schedule does not include

Trial protocols like this are developed for a specific research context with defined monitoring by clinical staff, not as general instructions for unsupervised use. Retatrutide remains investigational with no approved dosing schedule for use outside a clinical trial, and how any dose is escalated in practice should be guided by a healthcare professional who can account for individual response and tolerability.

## A simple, practical starting point

Retatrutide has no approved dose or official schedule, but a cautious and widely used approach for anyone new to it is to start at 1 mg, increase by 1 mg each week, and stop climbing once 4 mg is reached, maintaining that dose from then on. This keeps early GI side effects manageable while still building toward a dose that produces a noticeable effect, and it follows the same slow-and-steady logic the trials themselves were built around, without trying to replicate any specific trial arm.

## Why tolerability was central to escalation design

The Phase 2 trial recorded gastrointestinal side effects, primarily nausea, diarrhoea and vomiting, as the most common adverse events, particularly during the escalation period rather than once a stable maintenance dose was reached. This pattern, side effects concentrated around dose increases, is consistent with what has been reported for other GLP-1 based drugs during their own escalation phases.

## How this compares to related drugs

Tirzepatide's approved dosing as Mounjaro also uses a step-up schedule, generally increasing every four weeks, for the same tolerability reasons. The shared approach reflects common pharmacology principles across this drug class rather than something specific to any one compound.

## What retatrutide is

Retatrutide activates the GLP-1, GIP and glucagon receptors. In the Phase 2 trial, the highest dose group lost an average of 24.2% of body weight at 48 weeks, following the staged escalation schedule described. It remains in Phase 3 trials with no approval anywhere, and this article describes the trial's published methodology, not guidance for personal use.

## Bottom line

Retatrutide's Phase 2 trial used a roughly four-weekly staged dose increase, aligned with how long the drug takes to reach steady levels at each step and designed to manage tolerability as the dose rose. This reflects trial methodology under clinical supervision, not a personal dosing plan.
