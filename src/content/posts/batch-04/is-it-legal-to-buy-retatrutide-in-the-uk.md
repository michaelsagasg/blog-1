Retatrutide does not hold a licence from the MHRA, the UK's medicines regulator, and it has not been approved as a medicine anywhere in the world. It remains in Phase 3 clinical trials. That regulatory status is why it is sold by specialist sellers as a research product rather than dispensed by a pharmacy, and why the rules that apply to it are different from those that apply to a licensed medicine like Mounjaro.

## What "no licence" actually means

A medicines licence is granted after a regulator reviews trial data on safety and effectiveness for a specific use. Retatrutide's trial programme, run by Eli Lilly, is ongoing, and no regulator has completed that review yet. Without a licence, it cannot legally be marketed, prescribed or sold as a medicine for a specific health condition in the UK.

## How it is actually sold instead

UK sellers list retatrutide as a research chemical or research peptide, generally with wording that it is intended for research use rather than human consumption. That labelling reflects its regulatory status: it has not gone through the approval process required to be sold as a medicine for people to take.

## Why this differs from tirzepatide

Tirzepatide, sold as Mounjaro, completed its trial programme and received MHRA approval for specific uses, which is why it can be dispensed through a pharmacy with a prescription. Retatrutide has not reached that point, so no equivalent licensed pathway exists for it in the UK yet.

## What this means for a UK buyer

Because retatrutide sits outside the licensed medicines system, none of the protections that come with a licensed product, such as regulator-approved manufacturing standards or pharmacist oversight, apply to it. That is a central reason buyers are advised to look closely at a seller's own documentation, particularly a batch-specific certificate of analysis, since no regulator is independently verifying the product on their behalf.

## Rules and guidance can change

Regulatory status is not static. Trial results, safety data and regulator decisions can shift how a compound like retatrutide is classified and sold over time. For current, authoritative guidance on the legal status of buying, importing or possessing any specific substance in the UK, the MHRA and gov.uk are the primary sources, and they should be checked directly rather than relying on any single article, including this one.

## What retatrutide is, clinically

Retatrutide activates three receptors: GLP-1, GIP and glucagon. In Eli Lilly's Phase 2 trial, the highest dose group lost an average of 24.2% of body weight at 48 weeks. Those results are why there is strong interest in the compound ahead of any approval, but trial results are not the same as a licence, and a licence is what determines how a product can be sold and used.
