Tirzepatide needs a prescription when it is sold as Mounjaro, the licensed medicine dispensed through UK pharmacies after an assessment by a registered prescriber. When the same compound is sold unbranded as a research-grade peptide by specialist online sellers, it does not go through a prescription pathway at all, because that supply chain sits outside the licensed medicines system.

## Why the same drug has two different answers

A prescription is tied to a specific licensed product and supply chain, not just the underlying molecule. Mounjaro is licensed by the MHRA as a complete product: a specific formulation, manufacturer and pen device, reviewed and approved for defined uses. A peptide vial sold by a different supplier, even if it contains the same active compound, has not gone through that same product-specific licensing process, so no prescription pathway applies to it.

## The prescription route

To get Mounjaro, a patient completes an assessment, in person or online, with a registered prescriber who reviews suitability before authorising a pharmacy to dispense it. This route includes pharmacist oversight and the manufacturing standards required of a licensed medicine.

## The peptide-seller route

Outside that pathway, tirzepatide is sold as a research chemical or peptide, generally labelled for research use. No prescriber is involved, and no pharmacy dispenses it, which shifts responsibility for verification onto the buyer and the seller's own documentation, particularly a batch-specific certificate of analysis.

## What the trial data behind the licence shows

Tirzepatide activates the GLP-1 and GIP receptors. In Eli Lilly's SURMOUNT-1 trial, the highest dose group lost an average of around 21% of body weight over 72 weeks. That data supported its MHRA approval as Mounjaro for weight management and type 2 diabetes in eligible adults, which is the basis for the prescription requirement on that specific product.

## Checking the current position

Regulatory status and supply arrangements can shift over time. The MHRA and gov.uk are the authoritative sources on tirzepatide's current licensing and prescription requirements, and they are worth checking directly rather than relying on a single article for the latest position.
