Retatrutide's clinical trial protocols use once-weekly injections. That frequency is driven directly by its pharmacokinetics: a half-life of roughly six days means the drug stays present in the body long enough that a new dose every seven days keeps levels from swinging too sharply between injections.

## The link between half-life and injection frequency

A drug's half-life largely determines how often it needs to be given to keep levels reasonably steady. A short half-life drug, cleared within hours, would need multiple daily doses to maintain an effect. A long half-life drug like retatrutide, cleared over roughly a week, only needs weekly dosing to keep levels within a workable range.

## Why not more or less often

Injecting more frequently than once a week would build up higher levels than trial protocols were designed around, since the drug has not yet cleared much from the previous dose. Injecting less often than once a week would let levels fall further between doses, producing bigger swings between peak and trough. Weekly dosing sits at the frequency the trial data supports.

## How trial dosing was structured

Phase 2 trial protocols for retatrutide used a dose-escalation schedule, starting at a lower weekly dose and increasing at set intervals, generally every four weeks, up to a target maintenance dose. This staged approach let researchers assess tolerability as doses increased, rather than starting participants at the highest studied amount immediately.

## What "once a week" means for timing

Weekly dosing in trials generally allowed some flexibility around the exact day, typically within a day or two of the scheduled date, as long as consistency was maintained overall. This reflects general pharmacology principles around drugs with multi-day half-lives, not a personal recommendation, since any actual schedule is a decision for a clinician and does not apply to unlicensed research use.

## Why consistency matters more than exact timing

Because levels build gradually over several weeks to reach a steady state, an injection schedule that is roughly consistent week to week supports that build-up more reliably than one that varies widely. Significant gaps between doses can mean levels fall further than intended before the next dose arrives.

## What retatrutide is

Retatrutide activates the GLP-1, GIP and glucagon receptors and remains an investigational drug in Phase 3 trials, without approval anywhere. Its half-life and dosing schedule come from Phase 2 trial data published by Eli Lilly. This article explains the pharmacology behind the weekly schedule used in trials; it is not dosing guidance for personal use.

## Bottom line

Retatrutide's roughly six-day half-life is why trial protocols use once-weekly injections. This frequency keeps levels from swinging too sharply between doses while allowing a gradual, staged increase toward a maintenance amount over the trial's dose-escalation period.
