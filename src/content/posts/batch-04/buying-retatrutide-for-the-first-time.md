Your first retatrutide order in the UK will usually involve picking a vial size, checking the seller's certificate of analysis, paying by card or bank transfer, and waiting one to three days for tracked delivery. Knowing what each step looks like in advance makes the process far less confusing than going in cold.

## Choosing a vial size

Retatrutide is commonly sold in vials or pens ranging from around 2 mg up to 20 mg or more. There is no universal "right" size to start with. Some buyers prefer a smaller vial for a first order, both to limit spend while testing a new seller and to see how quickly it is used. Our guide to [retatrutide vial sizes explained](/posts/batch-04/retatrutide-vial-sizes-explained/) breaks down the common options.

## What a listing should show you before you order

- A certificate of analysis (COA) tied to the specific batch you will receive
- The vial size in mg, so you can work out price per mg
- A UK company name and contact details
- Clear delivery timeframes and a returns policy

If any of these are missing, it is worth pausing before you order rather than assuming they exist somewhere else on the site.

## Placing the order

Most UK sellers run a standard e-commerce checkout: add the product, enter delivery details, and pay by card, bank transfer or sometimes cryptocurrency. Card payment through a processor generally gives you more recourse through your bank if something goes wrong with the order.

## What happens after you pay

A well-run seller sends an order confirmation, followed by a dispatch notice with tracking once the parcel ships. Orders placed before an early afternoon cutoff are often dispatched the same day, with next-day delivery common for UK addresses via Royal Mail Tracked or a courier.

## What arrives

Expect discreet outer packaging that gives no indication of the contents from the outside. Because retatrutide typically needs refrigeration, look for insulated packaging or a cold pack inside, especially if you ordered during warmer weather. The [parcel arrival checklist](/posts/shop/retatrutide-parcel-arrival-checklist/) covers what to check in the first ten minutes after opening it.

## Matching the batch number

The vial should have a batch number printed on the label. Check this against the COA the seller showed on the listing before you ordered. If the numbers do not match, or there is no batch number at all, contact the seller before using the product.

## Storage from day one

Refrigerate the vial as soon as it arrives unless the listing states otherwise, and keep it away from light and heat. Label it with the date received so you can track how long it has been stored.

## Supplies to have ready

Before your order arrives, it helps to have fridge space, alcohol swabs, and a sharps disposal container on hand if you are planning to reconstitute and use the product. The [starter supplies shopping list](/posts/shop/retatrutide-starter-supplies-shopping-list/) has a fuller rundown.

## What you are buying

Retatrutide is an investigational drug, still going through Phase 3 trials and not approved for use anywhere. In Eli Lilly's Phase 2 study, participants on the highest dose lost an average of 24.2% of body weight at 48 weeks over the course of the trial. A first-time buyer should understand that a research-grade product carries none of the guarantees that come with a licensed medicine, and no seller can promise a specific personal outcome.

## Common first-order questions

**Do I need a prescription?** No UK pharmacy dispenses retatrutide, since it is not licensed anywhere yet. It is sold by specialist sellers as a research product.

**How long does delivery take?** Typically one to three working days for a UK order, depending on the seller's dispatch cutoff and courier.

**What if the batch number does not match?** Contact the seller directly before using the product and ask for clarification or a replacement.

## Bottom line

A first retatrutide order looks like any other online purchase: pick a size, check the COA and seller details, pay, and track the delivery. The parts that actually matter for safety are the batch-specific COA and the seller's transparency, not how polished the website looks.
