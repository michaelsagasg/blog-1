BAC water turns freeze-dried retatrutide powder into a liquid you can measure. The amount you add does not change how much drug is in the vial. It only changes how concentrated the liquid is, and that decides how many units you draw for a given dose.

Retatrutide is an investigational drug. It has no approval and no official patient instructions. This post explains the arithmetic and handling basics that apply to any lyophilized peptide. It is not medical advice, and it does not tell you what dose to take.

## What BAC water is

BAC water is bacteriostatic water: sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol. The alcohol slows bacterial growth. That matters because you pierce the rubber stopper many times over several weeks.

Sterile water for injection has no preservative. It is meant for single use. Once you pierce the stopper, the vial is exposed to whatever gets in, and nothing slows growth.

Saline is a different product. Do not swap it in for BAC water unless the source you are following says so.

## Why the powder needs a diluent

Manufacturers freeze-dry peptides because the dry powder is more stable than a liquid during shipping and storage. You add liquid right before use. The powder in a vial labeled 10 mg contains 10 mg of peptide (plus a small amount of filler). That number does not change when you add water.

## The one formula that matters

Concentration = total mg in the vial divided by mL of BAC water added.

| Vial | BAC water added | Concentration |
|---|---|---|
| 10 mg | 1 mL | 10 mg/mL |
| 10 mg | 2 mL | 5 mg/mL |
| 10 mg | 2.5 mL | 4 mg/mL |
| 15 mg | 3 mL | 5 mg/mL |

More water means a weaker liquid and a larger volume per dose. Less water means a stronger liquid and a smaller volume. Both hold the same total drug.

## Turning concentration into syringe units

Most insulin syringes are U-100. That means 100 units equals 1 mL, so 1 unit is 0.01 mL.

- At 10 mg/mL, 1 unit holds 0.1 mg.
- At 5 mg/mL, 1 unit holds 0.05 mg.
- At 4 mg/mL, 1 unit holds 0.04 mg.

To find units for a dose: dose in mg divided by mg per unit. A 2 mg dose at 5 mg/mL is 2 / 0.05 = 40 units. The same dose at 10 mg/mL is 2 / 0.1 = 20 units.

Pick a volume that gives round, easy-to-read numbers on your syringe. Very small draws, under 5 units, are hard to read accurately. A tiny misread is a large percentage error.

## How to add the water

1. Wash your hands and wipe both stoppers with an alcohol pad.
2. Draw the planned amount of BAC water into a syringe.
3. Insert the needle into the powder vial at an angle, so the stream runs down the glass wall.
4. Push the plunger slowly. Do not blast the powder.
5. Remove the needle and let the vial sit. Roll it gently between your fingers if needed.

Do not shake it. Shaking creates foam and can damage the peptide chain. Give it several minutes. The liquid should end up clear and free of particles. If it stays cloudy or has visible specks, do not use it.

## Storage after mixing

Once mixed, the vial goes in the refrigerator, usually 2 to 8 °C. Do not freeze the liquid. Keep it away from light and heat.

There is no published stability study for reconstituted retatrutide from a manufacturer, so any "use within X days" number is a convention, not a tested limit. Sources commonly cite about 28 days for a multi-use vial of bacteriostatic solution, but that figure comes from general practice with other injectables. Treat it as a ceiling, and discard early if the liquid changes color or clarity.

## Label everything

Write the date and concentration on the vial. Two weeks later, you will not remember whether you added 1 mL or 2 mL. Guessing wrong on concentration means every dose is wrong by the same factor.

## What the trials did differently

In clinical trials, participants got a prefilled, manufacturer-prepared product from a controlled source, with dose steps set by protocol. The phase 2 study used weekly doses of 1, 4, 8 and 12 mg, with some arms starting at 2 mg and stepping up. None of that involved anyone mixing powder at home.

That gap is the main risk. Purity, actual content per vial and sterility are not verified for powder from unregulated sellers. Reconstitution technique cannot fix a vial that contains the wrong amount of drug or contaminants.

## Key takeaways

- BAC water is sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol, used to dissolve the powder and slow bacterial growth in a multi-use vial.
- Total drug in the vial stays fixed. Water volume only sets concentration.
- Concentration = mg in vial / mL of water. On a U-100 syringe, 1 unit = 0.01 mL.
- Add water slowly down the vial wall, do not shake, and refrigerate after mixing.
- Label the vial with date and concentration.

If you are considering any injectable that is not approved, talk with a licensed clinician first. They can tell you what is known, what is not, and what to watch for.
